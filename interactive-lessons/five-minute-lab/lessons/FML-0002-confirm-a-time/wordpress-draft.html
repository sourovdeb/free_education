So, That's 7:30 on Thursday?

Goal: Confirm a booking time by selecting the risky details, arranging a checking question, and producing a new confirmation.

Your shuttle is booked for Thursday at 7:30 a.m. Pick-up is at the east entrance.

You think you heard the booking correctly. You want to check the day and time before ending the conversation.

HEAR: Thursday at 7:30 -> SELECT: day plus time -> REPEAT: 7:30 on Thursday -> CHECK: right?

So, that's 7:30 a.m. on Thursday, right?

Form

A conversational check can use So, + that's + detail + right? A neutral full question can use Is that + detail? Use at with a clock time and on with a day when stating the arrangement.

Meaning

The speaker repeats information they believe is correct and asks the listener to confirm it.

Appropriateness

Right? is conversational and can sound more or less certain depending on intonation. Is that ...? is a neutral alternative when speaking to an unfamiliar person.

Practice

Which sentence checks the booked day and time?

  • Is that 7:30 a.m. on Thursday? Yes. It repeats both risky details and asks for confirmation.
  • What time is it? This usually asks for the current time. It does not repeat the booking details.
  • See you sometime Thursday. This keeps the day but loses the exact time, so the booking remains partly unchecked.

Match each booking detail to its role.

  • Thursday → day. Correct. Thursday names the day.
  • 7:30 a.m. → time. Correct. 7:30 a.m. names the time.
  • east entrance → place. Correct. East entrance names the pick-up place.

Build the conversational checking question.

So, that's 7:30 a.m. on Thursday, right?

Complete: So, that's 7:30 a.m. on Thursday, right?
