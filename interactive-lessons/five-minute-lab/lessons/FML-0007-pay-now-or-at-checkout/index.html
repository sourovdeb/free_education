Pay Now or at Checkout?

Goal: Use Do I pay + option A + or + option B? to check a payment procedure and confirm the answer.

Older teenagers and adults. A2-B1 estimate. 3-5 minutes.

Prerequisites: Can understand simple hotel questions.; Knows present simple statements with pay.

The payment gap

Your room is booked. Reception has not said when payment happens.

Do I pay now or at checkout?

Payment path

ARRIVAL: pay now? | STAY | CHECKOUT: pay later? -> ASK: Do I pay now or at checkout? -> CONFIRM: I'll pay when I check out.

The gap

Your room is booked. Payment timing is unclear.

Two payment moments seem possible: arrival or checkout.

Meaning, form, context

Form: Do + subject + base verb forms a present simple question. After do, use pay, not paying.

Meaning: The speaker asks which of two payment times or procedures applies.

Time: At checkout means during the hotel leaving-and-billing stage. Here, now means before the stay begins.

Context: This direct choice question is suitable when both options seem possible. Should I pay...? is also common when asking what the hotel expects.

Try a cafe

At a cafe, you do not know whether to pay at the counter or at your table.

Write one two-option question. Then add one short confirmation for the answer: 'At the counter.'

Nothing is saved. Nothing is graded.

Show self-check
  • The question checks a real procedure.
  • The two options are clear.
  • The verb form is usable.
  • The confirmation matches the answer.
  • A valid alternative is accepted.

Possible answers:

  • Do I pay at the counter or at the table? Thanks. I'll pay at the counter.
  • Should I pay at the counter or at the table? Okay, at the counter.
  • Do we pay here or at the table? Thanks, we'll pay here.

Other clear answers can work.

Reading mode

This remains usable without scripts.

Which sentence clearly checks the procedure?

  • Do I pay now or at checkout? Yes. Do starts the question, pay stays in the base form, and both times are clear.
  • I pay now or at checkout. This has statement word order. Use Do I pay...? to ask the question.
  • Do I paying now or at checkout? After do, use the base verb pay, not paying.

Reception says, 'At checkout.' What does that answer tell you?

  • Pay when leaving the hotel. Yes. Hotel checkout is the stage when a guest leaves and settles the bill.
  • Pay before entering the room. No. That would mean pay now. At checkout points to the leaving stage.
  • Payment is optional. No. The answer gives the payment time. It does not remove the payment.

Which reply confirms what you understood?

  • Thanks. I'll pay when I check out. Yes. It acknowledges the answer and restates the payment time.
  • Thanks. I paid when I checked out. That describes a completed past event. Your stay has not happened yet.
  • Thanks. Checkout is optional. No. Reception described payment timing, not whether checkout is optional.
