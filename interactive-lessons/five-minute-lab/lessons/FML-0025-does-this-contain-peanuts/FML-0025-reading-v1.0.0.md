# Does This Contain Peanuts?

**FML-0025 · v1.0.0**  
**Audience:** Older teenagers and adults.  
**Level:** A2-B1 editorial estimate.  
**Time:** About 3-5 minutes.  

## Goal

State a food allergy, ask whether a dish contains that allergen, and ask staff to check preparation before ordering.

## Situation

You have a peanut allergy. You want vegetable curry.

## Model

1. **State:** “I have a peanut allergy.”
2. **Ask:** “Does this curry contain peanuts?”
3. **Check:** “Please check cross-contact before I order.”

“Contains” asks about ingredients. “Cross-contact” asks whether the allergen could reach food during preparation.

## Decision one

Which opening states the risk clearly?

- A. No peanuts.
- B. I have a peanut allergy. Does this curry contain peanuts?
- C. I do not like peanuts.

**Answer: B.** It names the allergy. It also asks a direct question. A is ambiguous. C states a preference.

## Decision two

Staff says: “The curry contains peanuts. The tomato soup has no peanut ingredients, but it is prepared in the same kitchen. I need to check cross-contact.”

What should you say?

- A. Great. Bring the soup.
- B. Please check cross-contact before I order.
- C. Then the curry is safe.

**Answer: B.** The preparation check remains unfinished.

## Your turn

You have an egg allergy. You want the house salad. The menu does not list dressing ingredients.

Write two sentences. State the allergy. Ask about the dressing. Add a preparation check if needed.

Possible answer:

> I have an egg allergy. Does the dressing contain egg? Please check how it is prepared before I order.

## Important limit

This lesson teaches communication. It cannot confirm food safety. Follow local guidance and your healthcare plan.
