# CT02 — Separate What Happened from Your Explanation

[Course index](../README.md) · Day 2 · SORT

## Today’s rule

An observation is not the same thing as an explanation. A feeling can matter without proving another person’s motive.

## Today’s task

Describe one recent event under three headings: “What I observed,” “What I think it means,” and “What I do not know.” Keep claims made by someone else identified as reports, not verified events.

## Why this matters

When an interpretation is silently promoted to a fact, the next decision can rest on something that was never established. Separating the two lets you investigate without dismissing your own experience.

## Hypothetical example

Observation: no reply has arrived for three days. Interpretation: “They are deliberately ignoring me.” Unknown: whether they read it or were able to reply. A defensible next step is a neutral follow-up, not an accusation about an unestablished motive.

## Self-check

Could someone agree with the observation but disagree with your explanation? Then they are distinct claims. Do not replace a negative interpretation with a comforting explanation and call that verified either.
