# Build Life Playbook

## Contract

Help users choose a direction. Preserve their authority over life. Offer hypotheses, not destiny claims. Ground recommendations in inspected evidence. Separate livelihood from human worth.

Deliver work during this turn. Schedule future work through automations. Never imply continuous background work. Never substitute scheduling for delivery.

## Inputs

Use existing context before asking. Identify the user's requested destinations. Record scope and existing authorization. Discover account tools when needed. Use personal-context where continuity matters.

Check these five decision-changing gaps:

- Current employment and income.
- Costs and financial urgency.
- Capacity after existing obligations.
- Evidence for competence and qualifications.
- Commitments, relationships, and care needs.

Unknowns may remain unknown. Do not invent them. Use conditional branches for uncertainty. Ask at most three questions. Proceed when questions are optional.

## Workflow

### 1. Establish the decision

Define the thirty-day decision first. Identify the twelve-month direction separately. Include care, relationships, livelihood, meaning. Keep activities without productivity requirements.

Treat health disclosures as constraints. Do not diagnose through project volume. Avoid changing treatment or medication. Browse authoritative guidance when relevant. Recommend clinician input for care decisions. Do not introduce crisis framing unprompted.

### 2. Inspect evidence

Read [evidence-and-privacy.md](references/evidence-and-privacy.md).

Search targeted account records. Read decisive messages beyond snippets. Prefer recipient replies over drafts. Prefer issuer records over CV assertions. Prefer payment receipts over invoices. Prefer user tests over artifact counts.

Separate these evidence states:

`reported -> documented -> attempted -> delivered -> used -> outcome`

This sequence is not guaranteed. Record the state actually observed. Never infer the next state.

Treat assistant reports as leads. Repetition supplies no independent corroboration. Search for counterevidence and newer records. Preserve conflicting claims with dates. State inspection and coverage limits.

Stop when retrieval stops changing decisions. Preserve inaccessible sources as gaps. Never describe an incomplete account-wide audit.

### 3. Inventory commitments

Include projects and recurring automations. Inspect schedules through private lookup. Compare titles against stored recurrence. Include correction and consumption time. Do not assume every run succeeds.

Assign each project one status:

`do`, `maintain`, `wait`, `use`, `stop-proposed`.

Explain restart or completion conditions. Recommendations do not authorize changes. Never pause unrelated schedules automatically. Never delete projects to reduce overload.

### 4. Select the direction

Read [decisions-and-capacity.md](references/decisions-and-capacity.md).

Find demand already demonstrated. Look for current employer conversations. Look for receipts and repeat use. Look for assets requiring little setup.

Choose one livelihood commitment. Choose one learning or contribution commitment. Use fewer when capacity requires. Allow identity breadth without calendar breadth.

Check alternatives before choosing. Compare employment, services, and products. Consider maintenance, access, and geography. Flag requirements without claiming disqualification. Do not fabricate buyers or opportunities.

Use gates, not personality scores. Avoid invented probabilities and salary forecasts. Label numerical thresholds as heuristics.

### 5. Make actions executable

For each action, record:

- Owner and desired outcome.
- First physical step.
- Time ceiling and cost ceiling.
- Dependency and authority requirement.
- Deadline with its source.
- Evidence defining completion.
- Failure branch and stopping condition.

Distinguish planning dates from legal deadlines. Never calculate deadlines without governing rules. Preserve urgent deadlines before timeboxing disputes.

Build three horizons:

1. Now: one twenty-minute action.
2. Thirty days: one decision test.
3. Ninety days: direction reassessment.

Add requested horizons when relevant. Do not require working through sleep. Preserve household and employment obligations.

### 6. Budget and validate

Use [plan-template.json](assets/plan-template.json).

Set capacity from user evidence. Otherwise mark it as assumed. Protect a reserve within capacity. Schedule after known availability constraints. Lost time creates no catch-up debt.

Count recurring review and maintenance costs. At low capacity, reduce experiments. At high capacity, preserve commitment limits. Expand depth before project count.

Run `scripts/validate_plan.py PLAN.json`.

A passed validator checks structure. It cannot establish life feasibility. Review assumptions and evidence separately.

### 7. Build deliverables

Create a layered playbook:

- Start page with three actions.
- Direction with evidence and alternatives.
- Project decisions with restart gates.
- Capacity and fallback week.
- Experiment with completion evidence.
- Review questions and change conditions.
- Sources, limitations, and receipts.

Use a file for long-form work. Use PDF when reading benefits. Render and inspect created PDFs. Use exact tables for mappings. Use accessible visuals when useful. Keep visuals free of invented data.

Follow the user's writing preferences. Avoid adding a new management platform. Reuse their working tools and files. Save artifacts through applicable storage workflows.

### 8. Deliver and verify

Read [delivery-and-recovery.md](references/delivery-and-recovery.md).

Resolve recipients before email delivery. Check repository visibility before uploads. Publish only the approved data scope. Keep sensitive details out of public repositories. Use private companion notes when necessary.

Honor authorization already provided. Do not demand redundant approvals. Execute requested delivery after validation. Read back repository content after writes. Retain message and commit receipts privately.

Schedule only requested future work. Confirm required app access first. Use finite schedules for finite requests. Include exact artifact paths for continuation. Include deduplication and recovery instructions.

Distinguish scheduled from executed. Distinguish saved from published. Distinguish email accepted from read. Report unresolved failures without fabricating completion.

## Stopping condition

Finish when deliverables are usable. Verify requested destinations and scheduling. Report decisive unknowns beside their consequences. Leave one next physical action. Do not finish with more projects.

## Evaluation

Use [evaluation-cases.md](references/evaluation-cases.md).

Test missing facts and conflicting claims. Test capacity cuts and failed delivery. Test privacy and publication boundaries. Test adaptation beyond one user's history.
