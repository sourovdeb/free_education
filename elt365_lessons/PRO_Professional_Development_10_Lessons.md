# 5-Min Pro — Professional Development Series (10 Lessons)

### P01 · How to Write a Lesson Aim Learners Can Actually Understand
**The idea —** A lesson aim is a promise to learners, not a note to the observer. If learners can't read it and know what they'll be doing, rewrite it.  
**In practice —** Bad aim: 'To practise language.' Better: 'By the end, you will be able to use three phrases for politely refusing invitations.' Measurable, specific, learner-facing.  
**One-minute task —** Take one aim you have written this week. Rewrite it so a learner could read it and say: 'I know exactly what I'm going to learn.'

### P02 · Setting Up a Teaching Portfolio
**The idea —** A teaching portfolio is evidence of development, not a collection of certificates. Observers want to see what you noticed, changed, and learned.  
**In practice —** Include: one lesson plan with a reflective annotation, one piece of anonymised learner feedback, one observation note, one thing you would do differently.  
**One-minute task —** Open a blank document. Write: 'What I noticed this week.' Write two sentences. That is your first portfolio entry.

### P03 · Peer Observation: Giving Feedback That Lands
**The idea —** Peer feedback fails when vague ('great energy'), when it's a list of everything, or when it arrives without anchor to evidence.  
**In practice —** 'When you asked the gist question before playing the audio, I noticed three learners who usually disengage lean forward.' Specific, evidenced, actionable.  
**One-minute task —** Next time you observe a colleague, write down one specific moment and what you noticed. That is your feedback.

### P04 · Reflective Teaching Journals: A Practical Start
**The idea —** Journals fail when people start too ambitiously. One question per week is sustainable. Three paragraphs per lesson is not.  
**In practice —** Pick one question and return to it every week for a month: 'When did learners talk most today, and why?' The pattern across four weeks is more useful than four separate essays.  
**One-minute task —** Write your question at the top of a blank page. Set a 5-minute timer. Write without stopping.

### P05 · How to Plan a 45-Minute Lesson from Scratch
**The idea —** A lesson without a plan is a sequence of activities. A plan answers: what will learners be better able to do at the end?  
**In practice —** Start with the aim. Write the final production task that proves they got there. Build backwards: what do they need to know to do that task? Those become earlier stages.  
**One-minute task —** Write your aim first. Then write the final task. Then stages. Timing comes last.

### P06 · Managing a Mixed-Ability Class
**The idea —** In a mixed-ability class, design one task with variable challenge — open the top and support the bottom. Not three separate tasks.  
**In practice —** One discussion question can be answered in one sentence (A2) or a complex paragraph (B2). Remove the word bank for stronger learners on a gap-fill.  
**One-minute task —** Take one task from your next lesson. Write one extension and one scaffold. Two sentences. Done.

### P07 · Assessment for Learning: Quick Tools
**The idea —** Assessment for learning uses information about what learners know to adjust teaching in real time. It is not a test at the end.  
**In practice —** Exit tickets: learners write one thing they learned and one thing they are still unsure about. Read them before the next class.  
**One-minute task —** Use an exit ticket next lesson. Last three minutes: 'I learned…' and 'I'm still not sure about…'

---

### P08 · Dealing with Disruptive Learners: Three Strategies
**The idea —** Most disruption is unmet need: boredom, confusion, or social need. The behaviour is the message. Read it before responding.  
**In practice —** Strategy 1: proximity — move near without stopping the lesson. Strategy 2: task adjustment — increase challenge or add collaboration. Strategy 3: private conversation after class.  
**One-minute task —** Next time a learner disrupts, pause two seconds and ask: boredom, confusion, or social need? Your response changes depending on the answer.

---

### P09 · Professional Development on a Budget
**The idea —** The most effective CPD often costs nothing and happens between lessons: peer observation, one article, one teaching video, reflecting on one moment.  
**In practice —** Free resources: IATEFL webinar recordings, Cambridge English Teacher community, Underhill Sound Foundations videos, Scott Thornbury's blog.  
**One-minute task —** Write one free CPD activity for this week. Schedule it for 30 minutes. That is your CPD.

---

### P10 · Error Correction: Deciding When and How
**The idea —** Not all errors need immediate correction. Interrupting a fluency task for accuracy correction kills communication.  
**In practice —** During fluency tasks, note errors. During accuracy tasks, correct in the moment. After fluency, write three errors (anonymised) on the board — elicit corrections from the class.  
**One-minute task —** In your next lesson, decide before each task: accuracy or fluency? Write it on your plan. That decision tells you exactly how to correct.
