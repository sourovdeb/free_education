# YL Series — Young Learners ELT (10 Lessons)

### YL01 · Teaching Young Learners: What's Different
**Idea —** Young learners (ages 6–12) respond to movement, repetition, and context rather than explanation. Grammar rules rarely stick; language embedded in a game or story often does.  
**Example —** An adult holds the rule 'use have got for possession' in working memory. A 7-year-old learns 'I've got a dog' by saying it twenty times while showing a picture card.  
**Try —** Plan one activity using movement, a game, or a story to convey meaning. No rule explanation. Notice whether the language sticks.

---

### YL02 · Songs and Chants in the Young Learner Classroom
**Idea —** Rhythm and repetition are among the most powerful mechanisms for language memorisation, especially for young learners.  
**Example —** A days-of-the-week chant teaches vocabulary, sequencing, and pronunciation simultaneously. Learners who can't write the words can sing them correctly after three repetitions.  
**Try —** Choose one target vocabulary set. Write a four-line chant with a clear beat. Practise it yourself before class.

---

### YL03 · Classroom Routines That Teach Language
**Idea —** Classroom routines are repeated language events. The same phrases every lesson become internalised without explicit teaching.  
**Example —** 'Good morning everyone. Sit down. Open your books to page…' said identically each lesson becomes the language learners expect, recognise, and eventually reproduce.  
**Try —** Identify three classroom routine phrases you use every lesson. Display them. Use exactly those phrases every class for one month.

---

### YL04 · Storytelling Techniques for Kids
**Idea —** Stories provide meaningful context for new language. A word inside a story is more memorable than the same word on a flashcard.  
**Example —** Tell a simple story: a character, a problem, a resolution. Use flashcard images. Stop at key moments: what happens next?  
**Try —** Plan a two-minute story for one piece of target language. Three elements: character, problem, happy ending.

---

### YL05 · Total Physical Response (TPR): How It Works
**Idea —** TPR (Asher, 1969) pairs language with physical action. Learners respond to commands with movement before producing language. This reduces anxiety and embeds meaning physically.  
**Example —** 'Stand up. Touch your nose. Sit down. Jump.' Once the sequence is secure, learners give the commands.  
**Try —** Design a 10-command TPR sequence for body parts, actions, or classroom objects. Run it as a warm-up.

---

### YL06 · Games That Teach Grammar to Kids
**Idea —** Grammar games create a need to use target structures to play. The game is the production task; the grammar is the tool learners use to participate.  
**Example —** 'Guess what's in my bag' generates 'Is it a…?' naturally. 'Simon says' practises imperatives.  
**Try —** Choose one grammar point. Find or adapt one game where learners must use that structure to play. Run the game first; name the language afterwards.

---

### YL07 · Managing Energy: Transition Activities
**Idea —** Young learner energy is a classroom resource, not a problem. Transition activities regulate energy between high-focus and physical activities.  
**Example —** After a running dictation (high energy), use a quiet colouring task (low energy) before reading.  
**Try —** Map your next YL lesson's energy levels: high, medium, low. Add a transition activity wherever two high-energy tasks appear in a row.

---

### YL08 · Assessment with Young Learners
**Idea —** Written tests rarely reflect what young learners know — they conflate language ability with writing ability. Observation and oral tasks give a truer picture.  
**Example —** While learners play a vocabulary game, note which children use target words correctly and which rely on pointing or L1.  
**Try —** Observe three specific learners next lesson and note one thing each can do with English that they couldn't do last month.

---

### YL09 · Parents as Partners in Young Learner Learning
**Idea —** Young learners spend more waking hours with their families than in class. Parent involvement multiplies contact time for English.  
**Example —** A weekly take-home card with three phrases for the parent to say with the child at bedtime requires ten seconds of teacher preparation and creates five minutes of daily practice.  
**Try —** Design one take-home activity: a game, a song, or three questions for parents to ask. Write it on a card. Usable with no instructions.

---

### YL10 · Digital Tools for Young Learner Classes
**Idea —** Digital tools in YL classes work best when they extend activity, not replace it. Screen time needs a clear pedagogical purpose.  
**Example —** Quizlet for vocabulary review, Google Slides for a digital word wall, Padlet for a class display — each has a specific function. One tool per lesson, not three.  
**Try —** Choose one digital tool you've never used with YL learners. Plan how to use it for exactly 5 minutes as part of a lesson where most time is away from screens.
