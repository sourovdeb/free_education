## P04 — Attention is a design condition

Not a generational diagnosis. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

### What evidence supports
PISA reports reading-performance declines. It studies fifteen-year-old students. It does not establish adult deficits. It cannot isolate one cause.

### What we will not claim
No universal attention timer is established here. We reject an eight-second curriculum. Not everyone dislikes reading.

### What we will test
Begin with one bounded task. Hide answers until an attempt. Offer breaks and return points. Extend practice after successful comprehension.

### Measure the right things
Record explanations, not watch-time alone. Compare drafts, not posting frequency. Ask learners about barriers.

Sources: R1, R3. See SOURCES.md.

## P05 — Chunks must lead to depth

Entry, practice, transfer. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

### Entry
One question opens the unit. Read a short source extract. Identify one decision or difficulty.

### Practice
Use a model and attempt. Return to the whole text. Add evidence and revise meaning.

### Transfer
Read an unseen passage. Write for a changed audience. Explain choices without automated help.

### Pilot duration bands
Try 3–6 minutes for entry. Try 15–25 minutes for practice. Try 30–45 minutes for transfer. These are adjustable design estimates. Accessibility needs take precedence.

Sources: R3, R5. See SOURCES.md.

## P06 — One loop, two audiences

Trainees and language learners. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

### Learner evidence
Read, locate, infer, explain. Plan, draft, revise, respond. Speak to negotiate intended meaning.

### Trainee evidence
Design tasks around those actions. Predict and diagnose a difficulty. Model one strategy explicitly. Observe responses before giving feedback.

### Do not mix clocks
Training may take thirty minutes. Its example may take five. Label both durations and outcomes.

### Return
Use a changed task later. Remove hints for that check. Retain access accommodations throughout.

## P08 — Reading: beyond answer hunting

Model the invisible decisions. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

### Understand
Set a reading purpose. Notice vocabulary and sentence relations. Identify references and implied meaning.

### Interrogate
Separate statement from inference. Locate the supporting sentence. Compare a second source. Notice absence and uncertainty.

### Teach
Model one difficult inference. Let learners attempt another. Ask what changed their interpretation.

### Assess
Use an unseen comparable passage. Ask for evidence and explanation. Do not assess speed alone. Audio support changes the measured construct; record its use.

Sources: D4, R2, R9. See SOURCES.md.

## P09 — Writing: meaning before polish

A reader must do something. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

### Design
Identify audience and purpose. Read a model for function. Plan the information order.

### Compose
Write before requesting AI help. Keep the draft and decisions. Use examples without copying meaning.

### Revise
Check the reader’s response. Address meaning and organisation first. Then inspect language and conventions.

### Assess
Use a changed purpose later. Ask the writer to explain. Preserve voice and intended meaning. Fluency is not only speaking.

Sources: D2, D5, R4. See SOURCES.md.

## P10 — Speaking supports literacy

Keep all four skills. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

### Before writing
Rehearse an explanation with someone. Ask what remains unclear. Use notes, not memorised paragraphs.

### After reading
Summarise without looking at sentences. Compare interpretations with a partner. Return to evidence after disagreement.

### After drafting
Ask a reader to respond. Record misunderstanding without blaming them. Revise the message accordingly.

### Listening and pronunciation
Use supplied scripts or recordings. Replay a troublesome stretch. Connect stress with intended meaning. Target intelligibility, not accent imitation.

Sources: D2, D6, R9. See SOURCES.md.

## P13 — AI assists; learners decide

Separate use from learning. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

### Before AI
Attempt the reading or draft. Record the difficulty in words. Select the assistance being requested.

### During AI
Request a hint or question. Check claims against the source. Reject invented evidence or meaning.

### After AI
Explain which suggestions were accepted. Revise using that explanation. Try another task without AI.

### Evidence balance
Research supports some designed tutors. Other designs harmed later performance. Neither result proves universal benefit. Our literacy design still needs trials.

## P15 — Skills and workflows differ

One procedure; one source. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

### Skill
Stores when and how to act. Loads only relevant supporting files. Produces a reviewable lesson candidate.

### Agent
Combines instructions, model and tools. It needs actual source access. A name grants no capabilities.

### Workflow
Coordinates repeated execution and state. It needs implementation and permissions. It is optional for the pilot.

### Publishing
Requires a separate explicit action. Verify the destination and receipt. Keep keys outside prompts. Avoid duplicate schedulers.

## P16 — Assess learning, not production

Separate four status fields. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

### Learning evidence
Baseline → supported attempt → delayed transfer. Compare task demands and conditions. Record source-use and revision quality.

### Teaching evidence
Observe lessons with real learners. Retain plans and feedback. Rehearsal does not count as teaching.

### Technical evidence
Passing checks confirms only their scope. It does not establish classroom effectiveness. It does not confer approval.

### Status
Work: candidate or reviewed. Validation: static or executed checks. Approval: pending or named decision. Deployment: not deployed or verified.

Sources: C2, D1, R6. See SOURCES.md.

## P17 — Two hundred hours: still provisional

An activity budget, not equivalence. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

### Allocation
Study and demonstrations: 40 hours. Observation: 20 hours. Planning and materials: 60 hours. Real teaching: 12 hours. Feedback and revision: 40 hours. Assessment and portfolio: 28 hours.

### Accounting
Count each activity once. Module work draws from this budget. Breaks and retries are recorded separately.

### Support
No twelve-hour placement is guaranteed. Mentors and learner groups need confirmation. Self-study cannot replace observed practice.

### Capacity example
Six trainees need 72 observation hours. Feedback time comes in addition. This arithmetic is not funded provision.

A bounded pilot. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

An internet-ready release. Version 3.0.0 · 2026-09-30 · Educator review pending.

### Private material

Keep Markdown and JSON as sources. Generate web pages and PDFs. Archive superseded versions without activating them.

### Future readiness
