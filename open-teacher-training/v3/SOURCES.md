# Evidence and source boundaries

Reviewed 30 September 2026. This is a focused review, not a preregistered systematic review. Research informs the curriculum candidate; it does not certify the sample lessons.

## Official requirements

**C1 — Cambridge CELTA syllabus, July 2024.** https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/21816-celta-syllabus.pdf

Pages 4–5 and 8–11 already include adult literacy, writing processes, online classrooms and digital materials. Five integrated topic areas are preserved. A specification does not establish actual delivery quality. We do not portray CELTA as unchanged since the 1990s.

**C2 — Cambridge CELTA course requirements.** https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/educators-organisations/professional-development/teaching-qualifications/celta/about/

At least 120 tutor-contact hours, plus approximately 80 additional learning hours; six assessed teaching hours and six directed-observation hours. Our 200-hour activity estimate is not an equivalence claim. Rehearsal is not real teaching.

**C3 — Cambridge generative-AI guidance.** https://www.cambridgeenglish.org/Images/745146-guidance-for-use-of-generative-ai-tools-on-celta-and-delta.pdf

Addresses AI use, attribution and centre guidance. It does not endorse this project or validate AI output.

## Supplied sources: originals excluded

**D1 — Candidate record booklet, C1/2026.** Course requirements, assignments, feedback and portfolio responsibilities were inspected. Private grades and personal records are not reproduced. This is not an appeal review.

**D2 — Neil Anderson, glossary of CELTA terms, February 2008.** The supplied A_GLOSSARY_OF_COMMON_CELTA_TERMS2.docx prioritises teaching over memorising terminology. MFPA, concept checks, instruction checks, fluency and feedback remain useful distinctions. Fixed encounter counts, minute caps and talk ratios are treated as local heuristics, not universal empirical laws. In this glossary, micro-teaching means support during monitoring; elsewhere it can mean a short rehearsal. The intended sense must be labelled.

**D3 — Blue Lesson Frameworks summary.** The summary allows adaptation and includes editing, correction and publishing in writing. Centre guidance is not identical to Cambridge regulations.

**D4 — Simon Brooks, Reading guidance, 2016.** Includes purposeful reading, detailed comprehension and text-based justification. Timings and vocabulary counts belong to an example template.

**D5 — Simon Brooks, Writing guidance, 2016.** Model texts, genre analysis, planning, supported composition and reader feedback. The supplied table was visually inspected. It is an illustrative 45-minute lesson, not the whole writing curriculum.

**D6 — Other supplied frameworks.** Listening, Speaking, grammar through text, lexis through text, functional language, situational lexis, and stage-name guidance. All seven were text-inspected. Not every layout was visually inspected. Guided discovery is distinguished from whole-lesson frameworks.

The internal page link could not be accessed. The novel-progress note contained status claims, not actual lesson assets. No Oxford curriculum was supplied. Missing material was not invented.

## Research

**R1 — OECD, PISA 2025 Results, Volume I, 2026.** https://doi.org/10.1787/73451bc5-en

The report documents declining reading performance. It studies fifteen-year-olds, not adult English learners. Associations do not isolate technology as a cause. We respond by prioritising comprehension and source evaluation, not generational stereotypes.

**R2 — Clinton, paper versus screen reading, 2019.** https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9817.12269

A meta-analysis of 33 studies found a paper advantage, particularly for expository texts. Task and design matter. This does not imply that online learning cannot work. Supply screen and print routes.

**R3 — Rey et al., segmenting effect, 2019.** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-018-9456-4

Across 56 investigations, meaningful learner-paced segments supported retention and transfer in multimedia learning. The study does not establish a universal five-minute lesson or eight-second attention limit. Segment meaning, then reconnect it into longer practice.

**R4 — IES, Teaching Secondary Students to Write Effectively, 2016/revised 2019.** https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/practiceguide/22

Model–Practice–Reflect, integrated reading/writing, and assessment-informed instruction. Population: grades 6–12. Adult ELT adaptation is a project inference, not a demonstrated effect in these packs.

**R5 — Agarwal, Nunes and Blunt, retrieval practice review, 2021.** https://doi.org/10.1007/s10648-021-09595-9

Classroom experiments generally support retrieval. Most included work came from WEIRD settings. Recall alone does not establish writing transfer. Include return tasks and changed-context performance.

**R6 — Bastani et al., generative AI and learning, 2025.** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122

A field experiment with nearly 1,000 Turkish high-school maths learners found improved assisted practice but weaker later unassisted performance with unrestricted assistance. Teacher-designed hints mitigated the harm. One school, mathematics and short-term outcomes; not a direct adult-writing result. Our design requires an attempt before hints and a later task without AI.

**R7 — Kestin et al., designed AI tutoring, 2025.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6

A randomised crossover trial found short-term benefits from a designed tutor in college physics. Two lesson topics and one university context. This is not evidence that any chatbot replaces teachers. It counters a blanket rejection of AI while reinforcing the need for careful design.

**R8 — UNESCO, AI competency framework for teachers, 2024.** https://www.iicba.unesco.org/fr/africa-education-knowledge-platform/ai-competency-framework-teachers

Human agency, ethics, foundations, pedagogy and professional learning. A competency framework, not a learning-gain trial. Teach judgement and privacy alongside tools.

**R9 — Council of Europe, CEFR Companion Volume, 2020.** https://www.coe.int/en/web/education/newsroom/-/asset_publisher/fkf5o6A9gZZK/content/id/63912058

Mediation, online interaction and plurilingual competence. The sample level labels are provisional; no formal CEFR validation occurred.

**R10 — Council of Europe, online interaction.** https://www.coe.int/en/web/common-european-framework-reference-languages/online-interaction-in-the-classroom

Connects online interaction with action-oriented language tasks. Use replies and collaboration, not clicks as proof of learning.

## Technology and access

**T1 — W3C WCAG overview.** https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/wcag/

Use testable criteria for keyboard access, reflow, contrast and alternatives. This release has not received a full conformance audit.

**T2 — W3C Use Clear Words.** https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o3p01-clear-words/

Explain unfamiliar terms and use clear labels. Supplemental guidance is not itself a conformance requirement.

**T3 — Mistral Studio skills.** https://docs.mistral.ai/getting-started/quickstarts/studio/create-skill

Skills store activation conditions, procedures and supporting files. Account UI, loading and permissions still need testing. Use one canonical skill and a thin adapter.

**T4 — Mistral Small 4.** https://docs.mistral.ai/models/mistral-small-4-0-26-03

Documented hosted candidate: mistral-small-2603. Total parameters are 119B; 6.5B active does not imply a 4GB-laptop deployment. No target-model benchmark ran.

**T5 — Ministral 3 3B.** https://docs.mistral.ai/models/model-cards/ministral-3-3b-25-12

Possible staged local route. Memory and latency depend on runtime, quantisation and context. No installation or speed result is claimed.

**T6 — Mistral durable agents.** https://docs.mistral.ai/studio/workflows/building-workflows/durable_agents

Workflow state and execution require implementation and permissions. A diagram is not a deployed worker. Automation remains optional for the first unit.

## Review discipline

Evidence, user information, inference, illustration and project proposals are labelled separately. Source retrieval is not source verification. AI review is not educator approval. Future-oriented choices are scenarios to test, not predictions presented as facts.
