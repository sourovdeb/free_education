Forty one-page stories, in two sequential parts, with simple English, original ASCII infographics and inspected sources.

The remaining 39 episodes are scheduled hourly from **24 September 2026 at 10:00** through **26 September 2026 at 00:00**, Indian/Reunion (UTC+4), including overnight. Part II is scheduled to begin on **25 September at 05:00**, provided prior deliveries succeed. A scheduled run is not a guarantee of successful upload or email: completion is recorded only after verification.

## Reading approach

Each page contains a short true story, an explicitly labelled imagined analogy where useful, two compact ASCII visuals, what changed and who bore the costs, an evidence limit, and a question to discuss together. Thoughtful speculation means exploring alternatives; skepticism means checking evidence. Neither requires taking financial risks. Religious claims are described as beliefs; historical and present-day geopolitical claims require evidence. Dynasties and religions do not explain everything: institutions, resources, geography and ordinary people matter too.

Prehistory begins with what archaeology can support, not invented names or a supposed first religion. Later stories distinguish direct continuity from analogy and political reuse of the past. Financial stories distinguish bubbles, crashes, banking crises and monetary turning points.

## Publication format

Every completed episode has `README.md` and an offline, mobile-readable `index.html` with print CSS. The HTML is a downloadable page, not evidence that GitHub Pages has been deployed. Open the downloaded HTML in a browser; use the browser's Print command. The numbered references link directly to sources. Source inspection limits are labelled. No external assets, fonts, libraries, tracking or account requirements.

The machine-readable queue is [series.json](series.json). Only educational topics and generic publication/delivery states belong in that public file. Keep personal email addresses, email message IDs, credentials and private context out of it. Preserve existing work; update only this series' files. Check sent mail before repeating a delivery. Report missing outputs honestly. If a channel fails, preserve the completed page and retry only the missing step.
