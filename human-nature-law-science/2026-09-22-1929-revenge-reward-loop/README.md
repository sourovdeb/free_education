# Page 1

## The buried trait

**The disturbing truth:**

Revenge can feel rewarding.

**What this does NOT prove:**

Revenge is not irresistible.

Revenge follows perceived wrongs.

It targets an offender.

It differs from justice.

Justice uses public rules.

Revenge stays personal.

Still, they can overlap.

### The basic mechanism

A grievance gets encoded.

Anger raises approach motivation.

The offender gains salience.

Retaliation gains expected value.

Opportunity becomes rewarding.

Action can bring relief.

Relief may stay brief.

Rumination can then continue.

That can restart anger.

### What research shows

De Quervain studied punishment.

Participants punished norm violators.

Some paid personal costs.

Dorsal striatum activity increased.

That region processes rewards.

Stronger activity predicted spending.

This was norm punishment.

It was not revenge.

That distinction matters.

Threadgill tested revenge opportunities.

Participants received insulting feedback.

They then competed again.

Revenge wins changed RewP.

RewP indexes reward processing.

Revenge wins produced more.

Anger shaped this response.

Eder tested punishment reactions.

Revenge-seekers showed positive shifts.

The effect stayed conditional.

No punishment often won.

So revenge stays mixed.

Carlsmith found another pattern.

People expected revenge relief.

Punishers often kept ruminating.

They sometimes felt worse.

So reward stays temporary.

### The uncomfortable point

Humans condemn revenge.

Humans can reward it.

Both can be true.

Moral judgment remains separate.

Neural reward means neither.

It means motivation changed.

# Page 2

## Law meets science

Law separates categories.

Science maps mechanisms.

Those tasks differ.

### Cooling time doctrine

Provocation can mitigate homicide.

But timing matters.

Courts ask cooling time.

State v. Soto illustrates.

The court separated revenge.

It treated revenge differently.

Planning also mattered there.

The doctrine assumes distinction.

Hot passion differs legally.

Cool revenge carries culpability.

Science complicates this line.

Planning can stay emotional.

Rumination can preserve anger.

Rehearsal can strengthen scripts.

Deliberation can coexist emotionally.

That creates the tension.

### What law needs

Law needs responsibility rules.

Law needs decision thresholds.

Law needs case judgments.

### What science offers

Science offers group patterns.

Science offers risk mechanisms.

Science offers uncertainty ranges.

It cannot assign guilt.

## Criminal investigation lens

Retaliation can spread violence.

Jacobs studied street retaliation.

They interviewed 52 offenders.

Anger distorted strike intensity.

Uncertainty distorted target choice.

Time pressure distorted choices.

Retaliation sometimes got redirected.

That can widen conflicts.

A 2025 forensic study

examined family homicide reports.

Only twenty cases appeared.

Revenge cases differed somewhat.

But the sample stayed small.

### Investigative implication

Test the grievance chain.

Test target substitutions.

Test revenge planning.

Test repeated rehearsal.

Test changing opportunities.

Do not infer guilt.

Emotion proves little alone.

## Political history lens

Private vengeance once governed.

Courts changed that structure.

Ancient Greek justice shows.

Anger entered public process.

State authority replaced vendetta.

But anger did not vanish.

Punishment absorbed parts instead.

This matters politically.

States monopolize retaliation.

They call it punishment.

Rules then constrain it.

## Nietzsche lens

Nietzsche studied ressentiment.

He treated resentment genealogically.

Moral values could emerge.

Injury could become judgment.

That is philosophy.

It is not evidence.

Research tests narrower mechanisms.

## Robert Greene lens

Greene studies power conflicts.

He treats retaliation strategically.

That lens predicts escalation.

It can aid interpretation.

It cannot prove causation.

His books stay non-scientific.

## Counterargument

Punishment can support cooperation.

Norm enforcement can help groups.

Retribution can feel legitimate.

Private revenge differs, though.

It bypasses shared procedure.

It can widen conflict.

## Method limits

Lab stakes stay artificial.

Brain signals stay indirect.

Reverse inference remains risky.

Cultures differ in revenge.

Samples often stay narrow.

Street interviews are selective.

Case studies stay small.

Revenge does not equal violence.

Violence has many causes.

## Bottom line

Revenge can recruit reward.

That reward can fade.

Rumination can remain.

Law separates hot passion.

Science sees blended states.

Responsibility still requires law.

Mechanism still requires science.
