# Human Nature Dossier

## Just-World Repair

**Date:** 2026-09-24 03:27

**Central question:**  
Why does injustice threaten us?

**Terminology note:**  
“Just-world repair” is shorthand.  
Researchers use just-world theory.

## Page 1 — The mechanism

We expect moral order.

Good should meet good.
Wrongdoing should meet costs.

Innocent suffering breaks this.

The mind seeks repair.

Sometimes repair stays external.

We help victims.
We compensate victims.
We punish offenders.

Sometimes repair turns inward.

We reinterpret victims.
We reinterpret outcomes.
We reduce apparent innocence.

This restores psychological order.

It can distort judgment.

### Foundational evidence

Lerner and Simmons tested this.

Seventy-two women participated.
A learner appeared shocked.
Observers could not intervene.

Continued suffering changed judgments.
Victim ratings became worse.

The classic study mattered.

Later research became mixed.

Dawtry and colleagues reviewed it.
They meta-analyzed 55 experiments.

Vivid contexts mattered more.
Realistic suffering increased derogation.

Older studies were stronger.
Year also confounded vividness.

Two new experiments followed.
Vivid presentations increased derogation.

So context matters.

Victim derogation is conditional.
It is not automatic.

### Other repair routes

Derogation is one route.

Other routes exist.

People may offer help.
They may demand restitution.
They may punish wrongdoers.
They may deny injustice.
They may reinterpret causes.

Hafer and Bègue reviewed these.
They stressed multiple strategies.

This matters morally.

Justice motives can help.
They can also harm.

### The disturbing truth

**The disturbing truth:** We may repair injustice psychologically.

That repair can blame.
It can preserve worldview.
It can reduce compassion.

### What this excludes

**What this does NOT prove:** Victims cause their suffering.

It proves no inevitability.
It proves no universal bias.
It proves no fixed personality.

It never proves guilt.

## Page 2 — Five lenses

### 1. Psychology

Just-world beliefs vary.

Self-beliefs differ from other-beliefs.

Bègue and Bastounis tested this.
They ran five studies.
Their total sample was 666.

Other-focused just-world belief mattered.
It correlated with poverty stigma.
It correlated with punitiveness.

Self-focused belief differed.
It related to life meaning.

That distinction matters.

The belief has benefits.
It can support planning.
It can support meaning.

That is the counterweight.

#### Counterargument

Justice beliefs are useful.

They support long-term action.
They can motivate helping.
They can motivate punishment.

Those functions are real.

Victim blaming is not required.

People can restore justice externally.
They can compensate victims.
They can correct institutions.

#### Method limits

Classic studies used deception.
Samples were often narrow.
Many studies use vignettes.

Recent results remain mixed.

Dawtry found strong moderation.
Vividness changed observed effects.

Older studies looked stronger.
Historical change could matter.

Measurement also matters.

Relative judgments increase derogation.
Absolute judgments can reduce it.

Causality needs careful designs.

No single mechanism dominates.

### 2. Criminal investigation

Investigators judge credibility.

That judgment matters greatly.

NIJ-supported research shows this.

Campbell and colleagues interviewed investigators.
Forty-four investigators participated.

Extralegal factors influenced credibility.
Victim behavior mattered.
Moral character mattered.

O’Neal studied LAPD cases.
Character judgments predicted skepticism.

Mental-health labels mattered.
Reputation concerns also mattered.

These findings are serious.

They do not prove causation.
They do not prove just-world motives.

But the overlap matters.

Just-world repair predicts reinterpretation.
Police research shows reinterpretation risks.

#### Investigation implication

Separate evidence from character.

Record factual contradictions precisely.
Do not moralize them.

Test competing explanations.
Seek independent corroboration.
Review language before closure.

NIJ reported another concern.
Report wording can propagate bias.

Early impressions can travel.
Later decisions can inherit them.

### 3. Political history

Britain changed poor relief.

The year was 1834.

National Archives documents this.

Public costs had risen.
Suspicion had also risen.

Some taxpayers blamed laziness.

Workhouses became central.
Conditions were deliberately harsh.
Help required institutional entry.

Families could be separated.
Work was compulsory.

The law faced opposition.

Critics saw punishment instead.
Poverty was often involuntary.

These are documented facts.

The psychological reading differs.

Just-world theory offers interpretation.
Deservingness can restore order.

But institutions lack minds.

This history proves nothing psychological.
It shows a pattern.

Misfortune becomes moralized.
Policy then reflects deservingness.

### 4. Nietzsche lens

Nietzsche questioned moral desert.

He studied punishment genealogically.

Debt shaped his account.
Power shaped his account.

Responsibility came later.

His claim was philosophical.
It was not experimental.

Applied here carefully:

Desert stories can reassure.
Punishment can restore balance.

Nietzsche challenges that comfort.

Science tests behavior.
Nietzsche questions moral origins.

These tasks stay separate.

### 5. Robert Greene lens

Greene emphasizes hidden motives.
He emphasizes self-deception.

He recommends emotional distance.
He recommends studying masks.

Applied here cautiously:

Fairness stories protect identity.
Blame can simplify chaos.

That resembles Greene’s themes.

It is not evidence.
Greene cannot establish causation.

Research carries that burden.

## Legal conflict

Law seeks relevant proof.

Character can distort factfinding.

Federal Rule 404 recognizes this.
It limits propensity character evidence.

Its notes explain why.
Character can overpersuade jurors.
It can distract factfinding.

Rule 412 goes further.

It covers sexual-misconduct cases.
It restricts sexual-history evidence.
It restricts predisposition evidence.

The goal includes fairness.
The goal includes privacy.
The goal includes stereotype control.

This creates tension.

Law can exclude evidence.
Minds can still infer.

Just-world repair predicts inference.

A victim seems unlucky.
That threatens moral order.
Then character becomes tempting.

Legal rules resist this.
Psychology explains the pressure.

### Homicide relevance

Homicide cases amplify stakes.

Victim character may arise.
Self-defense can raise exceptions.

Rule 404 permits limits.
Specific exceptions still exist.

The same danger remains.

Character can replace evidence.
Deservingness can replace causation.

Investigators should separate both.
Jurors should receive safeguards.
