# Investigation

Investigators face credibility decisions.
Confidence can grow quickly.
Evidence may not follow.
One study compared groups.
Professionals reached 60 percent.
Students reached 63 percent.
The difference was nonsignificant.
Other studies show variation.
Experience alone is insufficient.
Early impressions shape priorities.
Initial labels can harden.
Witness claims can spread.
Demeanor can mislead.
Nervousness has many causes.
Calmness proves little.
The better response changes focus.
Ask open questions.
Seek checkable details.
Separate facts from impressions.
Record first accounts.
Test competing explanations.
Revisit conflicting evidence.
Interview research offers methods.
Some techniques improve discrimination.
They increase usable information.
They still have limits.
Current guidance stresses science.
That direction matters.
The implication is practical.
Detect evidence, not vibes.
Credibility is not posture.
Suspicion is not proof.
Truth-default creates vulnerability.
Over-suspicion creates another danger.
Both errors can harm.

Evidence: Expert judgment stays limited.

Interpretation: Generate checkable information.

Uncertainty: No method guarantees truth.
