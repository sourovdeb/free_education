# Evidence And Limits

The effect has support.
The evidence also varies.
Bond and DePaulo pooled studies.
Average accuracy stayed near chance.
Their sample was large.
Yet tasks were constrained.
Many used balanced lies.
Real life differs.
Truth usually dominates communication.
That changes optimal guessing.
Clare and Levine added nuance.
They avoided constant prompting.
People rarely questioned veracity spontaneously.
That supports passive acceptance.
Other work adds limits.
Training can improve detection.
The gains are modest.
Verbal cues help more.
Nonverbal stereotypes help less.
Cognitive interviewing also helps.
Some studies reached better accuracy.
These methods create information.
They do not read minds.
High stakes can differ.
Familiar people can differ.
Cultural cues can differ.
Motivation can differ.
Base rates also matter.
Researchers still debate mechanisms.
Some prefer response-bias accounts.
Others favor truth-default theory.
Both predict truth judgments.
They differ in explanation.
No single cue works.
No facial sign proves lying.
Replication remains important.
Field validation also matters.

Evidence: Meta-analysis supports low accuracy.

Interpretation: Trust can be adaptive.

Uncertainty: Field settings differ.
