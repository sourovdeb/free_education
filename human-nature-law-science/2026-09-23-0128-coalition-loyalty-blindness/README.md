# Human Nature Dossier 06

## Coalition loyalty blindness

**Series time:** 2026-09-23 01:28.

**Core question:**

Can loyalty bend fairness?

Yes, sometimes it can.

Groups change moral judgment.

They change punishment too.

They change witness behavior.

They change self-judgment.

The direction can flip.

Sometimes insiders get leniency.

Sometimes insiders get punished.

The group image matters.

That is the problem.

# Page 1

## The buried mechanism

Humans form social coalitions.

Coalitions provide protection.

Coalitions provide identity.

Coalitions provide status.

Coalitions provide shared rules.

Those benefits create pressure.

Loyalty can shape perception.

The same act shifts.

Group membership changes interpretation.

Context changes punishment.

Research shows two patterns.

Pattern one is favoritism.

Insiders may get leniency.

Outsiders may get punishment.

Pattern two reverses it.

Insiders can get punished.

Why punish insiders harder?

They threaten group identity.

They damage group reputation.

Punishment restores group boundaries.

So loyalty is complex.

It is not kindness.

It is group maintenance.

## Psychology lens

Bernhard studied third-party punishment.

Participants protected ingroup victims.

Outgroup victims got less.

Shared groups changed punishment.

Violators expected ingroup leniency.

That changed norm violations.

Baumgartner studied similar choices.

Brain imaging was included.

Outgroup defectors got punished.

Ingroup defectors got leniency.

Mentalizing increased for insiders.

Interpretation may shield insiders.

But another pattern exists.

Marques called black-sheep effects.

Deviant insiders faced rejection.

Tang tested morality directly.

Moral violations mattered most.

Cohesion concerns explained punishment.

So loyalty can protect.

Loyalty can also expel.

The group decides direction.

## The disturbing truth

Fairness can follow membership.

## What this proves

Group identity affects judgment.

It affects punishment choices.

It affects social interpretation.

## What this does NOT prove

Groups always corrupt judgment.

Insiders always get leniency.

Outsiders always get punished.

Identity predicts no individual.

Coalitions excuse no crime.

It changes investigative risk.

Criminal groups also cooperate.

Cooperation became more likely.

Again, causation stays limited.

Investigators need independent evidence.

Statements require source checking.

Silence needs multiple explanations.

Loyalty should not be assumed.

Threats should not be assumed.

Both require evidence.
