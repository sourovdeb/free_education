## The buried mechanism

Authority supplies the rule.

Decision ownership moves outward.

Responsibility can then shift.

That shift can feel real.

Yet choice may remain.

This creates conflict.

Science measures felt agency.

Law assigns legal responsibility.

Morality assigns personal blame.

Institutions assign role duties.

These answers can differ.

## The disturbing truth

**Orders can shrink felt authorship.**

People may still act.

People may still choose.

Yet actions feel outsourced.

That feeling changes judgment.

It can change memory.

It can change blame.

It can change resistance.

## What authority outsourcing means

Authority outsourcing has layers.

One layer is obedience.

Another is responsibility displacement.

Another is agency reduction.

Another is goal identification.

These are not identical.

They often get mixed.

That causes bad claims.

Obedience is behavior.

Agency is felt control.

Responsibility is moral attribution.

Liability is legal judgment.

Identification means shared goals.

Each needs separate evidence.

## Classic obedience evidence

Milgram studied authority obedience.

His method used deception.

Participants faced escalating prompts.

Many continued despite protest.

In one baseline condition,

65 percent reached maximum.

That result became famous.

But interpretation remains contested.

Burger tested partial replication.

His design stopped earlier.

Seventy percent continued onward.

The checkpoint was 150V.

That was not 450V.

This distinction matters.

Doliński repeated that approach.

Seventy-two of eighty continued.

Again, the endpoint differed.

Ethics prevent exact repetition.

## Agency experiments

Caspar tested coerced actions.

Participants could cause harm.

Harm stayed experimentally bounded.

Coercion changed time perception.

Action-outcome intervals felt longer.

Neural outcome processing decreased.

Researchers linked this agency.

Akyüz later replicated this.

The design changed context.

The order was controlled.

Forced choices still stretched intervals.

Perceived autonomy also dropped.

This supports one mechanism.

It proves nothing universal.

## Why this unsettles

We prize personal authorship.

Law also needs authorship.

Morality needs blame targets.

Institutions need compliance.

Authority can blur ownership.

That creates social discomfort.

We want clean agency.

Human agency is contextual.

## Lens 3: Law

Law cannot measure feelings.

Law needs action standards.

It asks normative questions.

Was choice legally available?

Was coercion legally sufficient?

Was the order lawful?

Was illegality obvious?

### Legal conflict

Science finds reduced agency.

Law still assigns responsibility.

Nuremberg Principle IV matters.

Superior orders do not excuse.

A moral choice matters.

Rome Statute narrows exceptions.

Article 33 sets conditions.

Three conditions must coexist.

Legal obedience must exist.

Illegality must be unknown.

Illegality cannot be manifest.

Some crimes exclude defense.

This shows the conflict.

Felt agency can shrink.

Legal accountability may remain.

Science describes experience.

Law decides responsibility.

Those tasks differ.
