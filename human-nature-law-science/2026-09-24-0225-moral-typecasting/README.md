# Human Nature Dossier

## Moral Typecasting

**Date:** 2026-09-24 02:25

**Central question:**
Why do moral roles harden?

## Page 1 — Mechanism

Moral scenes feel binary.

Someone causes harm.
Someone receives harm.

Brains simplify this.
Researchers call this typecasting.

An "agent" acts.
A "patient" receives.

These roles can stick.

Then perception shifts.

Agents seem more capable.
Patients seem more vulnerable.

But something else happens.

Agents seem less vulnerable.
Patients seem less agentic.

That tradeoff matters.

Gray and Wegner tested it.
They ran seven studies.
The pattern appeared repeatedly.

Moral actors looked tougher.
Moral recipients looked weaker.

Both directions occurred.
Good agents showed it.
Bad agents showed it.

This was unsettling.

Virtue did not protect.
Role mattered instead.

Later work extended this.
Crime judgments showed effects.
Punishment could rise accordingly.

Miller and Borgida tested this.
Prior moral roles mattered.
Punitive judgments then shifted.

This exceeded simple deservingness.
Agency itself influenced punishment.

### The disturbing truth

**The disturbing truth:** Moral labels change perception.

They can outlive evidence.
They can reshape compassion.
They can reshape blame.

This threatens self-image.

We imagine fairness differently.
We picture neutral judgment.
Research shows shortcuts instead.

### What this excludes

**What this does NOT prove:** People ignore evidence.

It proves no inevitability.
It proves no determinism.
It proves no universal bias.

Role labels remain useful.
Victims need protection.
Offenders can cause harm.

The problem is rigidity.

## Page 2 — Five lenses

### 1. Psychology

Moral typecasting is relational.

People seek moral structure.
They often form dyads.

One side acts.
One side receives.

This reduces ambiguity.
It also hides complexity.

A victim can act.
An offender can suffer.

That overlap is common.

A 2012 review examined it.
Thirty-seven studies were reviewed.
Thirty-one supported overlap.
Six showed mixed support.

The finding crossed methods.
It crossed settings too.

Homicide research also matters.
Broidy and colleagues studied cases.
They found substantial overlap.
Contexts and risks overlapped.

Still, differences also existed.
Some victims remained distinct.

That nuance matters.

A later review agreed.
Major questions remain unresolved.
Methods still vary.
Causal pathways remain debated.

#### Method limits

Direct replications are limited.
FLoRA lists none currently.
That does not refute findings.
It lowers certainty instead.

Many studies use vignettes.
Many samples are Western.
Effects may depend context.
Role wording also matters.

Recent work supports extensions.
Yet mechanisms need testing.

#### Counterargument

Role categories aid reasoning.
They can clarify responsibility.
They can guide protection.

That is true.

Science objects only here.
Categories can become totalizing.

People contain mixed roles.
Events contain mixed causes.

### 2. Criminal investigation

Investigations need working theories.
That is unavoidable.

But theories can harden.

NIJ discusses tunnel vision.
Investigators may overfocus suspects.
Contrary facts may recede.

Moral typecasting could amplify this.
This link remains inferential.

A suspect becomes "agent."
A victim becomes "patient."

Then suspect vulnerability fades.
Victim agency may fade.

This can affect interviewing.
It can affect evidence weighting.
It can affect alternative theories.

Homicide cases need caution.
Victim-offender overlap exists.
That never means blame.
It means complexity exists.

#### Investigation implication

Keep competing hypotheses alive.
Record disconfirming evidence too.
Separate role from evidence.
Revisit first impressions often.

Independent review can help.
Disclosure rules also matter.

### 3. Political history

History shows role framing.

Nazi propaganda provides evidence.
USHMM documents this clearly.

Propaganda cast Jews conspiratorially.
They were framed as threats.
Germany was framed defensively.

Those claims were false.
The framing was political.

This resembles typecasting structurally.
It does not prove causation.

One group becomes "agent."
Another becomes "patient."

Complex history then disappears.
Moral permission can shift.

This is interpretation only.
USHMM supplies documented facts.
Psychology supplies the model.

### 4. Nietzsche lens

Nietzsche distrusted moral simplification.

His genealogy questioned punishment.
He traced changing meanings.
Debt shaped earlier punishment.
Power shaped moral concepts.

His point was philosophical.
It was not experimental.

Applied here carefully:
Moral roles may solidify.
Then punishment feels natural.

Science tests perception.
Nietzsche interrogates meanings.
These are different tasks.

### 5. Robert Greene lens

Greene studies social roles.
He stresses self-image heavily.

He also discusses masks.
He discusses defensive identity.

Applied here carefully:
People defend moral categories.
Those categories support self-image.

This remains interpretation.
It is not evidence.

Greene offers strategy prose.
Psychology supplies empirical tests.

## Legal conflict

Law separates formal roles.
Defendant and victim differ.

That separation is necessary.
Rights depend upon it.

But human lives overlap.
Past roles can differ.
Situations can reverse roles.

Lay punishment can shift.
Moral typecasting predicts this.

That creates legal tension.

Punishment should track evidence.
It should track culpability.
It should track proportionality.

Yet moral categories interfere.
They can influence jurors.
They can influence observers.

Miller and Borgida showed this.
Prior roles changed punishment.
Character valence was insufficient.
