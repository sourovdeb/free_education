# Page 1 — Moral Exclusion

## Definition

Moral exclusion narrows justice.

Some people remain protected.

Others leave that circle.

Rules can still exist.

Their reach simply shrinks.

Susan Opotow formalized this.

Her 1990 paper matters.

It defined exclusion broadly.

Targets can seem undeserving.

Targets can seem expendable.

Harm can seem acceptable.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-4560.1990.tb00268.x

## Two mechanisms

Later work split meanings.

Olson and colleagues tested two.

First, rules stop applying.

Second, harm feels deserved.

Those routes differ.

Both can change treatment.

That distinction matters legally.

Intent may differ too.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11211-011-0141-8

## Crime judgment evidence

Passini and Villano tested this.

They studied 255 adults.

Participants judged crime scenarios.

Nationality changed between versions.

Some participants scored exclusionary.

They judged Romanians harsher.

Italians received softer judgments.

Inclusive participants differed less.

That supports double standards.

It does not prove courts.

It does not prove policing.

It shows judgment risk.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.21500/20112084.3262

## Why exclusion happens

Threat can shrink concern.

Distance can shrink concern.

Deservingness also matters.

Utility can matter too.

Similarity often expands concern.

Contact may expand concern.

Institutions can resist narrowing.

Institutions can also reinforce it.

Research supports multiple routes.

No single route dominates.

## Nearby mechanisms

Moral exclusion overlaps prejudice.

It overlaps dehumanization.

It overlaps moral disengagement.

It overlaps victim blaming.

They are not identical.

That distinction matters.

A target can remain human.

Fairness can still disappear.

Michel makes this point.

Moral status differs conceptually.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1177/13540661241226474

Tileagă adds another warning.

Language can perform exclusion.

Descriptions create moral distance.

Labels can normalize distance.

Source:
https://doi.org/10.1348/014466607X186894

## Why this disturbs

Most people reject cruelty.

Most endorse equal justice.

Yet concern has boundaries.

Those boundaries can move.

Threat can change them.

Group labels can change them.

Deservingness can change them.

The shift can feel moral.

That is the discomfort.

People can feel righteous.

Standards can still narrow.

## What scientists dispute

The construct stays broad.

That creates measurement problems.

Some studies use scales.

Some use punishment judgments.

Some use language analysis.

Some use historical material.

Those methods answer differently.

Causation also varies.

Laboratories simplify conflict.

Field research loses control.

History cannot test mechanisms.

Replication is often conceptual.

Exact replications remain limited.

Samples often remain Western.

Self-report remains common.

Scenario studies reduce stakes.

## Counterargument

Not every distinction discriminates.

Different duties can exist.

Parents owe children more.

Judges weigh different facts.

Police prioritize urgent threats.

Law permits classifications sometimes.

So unequal treatment alone fails.

Context can justify differences.

Evidence must show arbitrariness.

The mechanism is probabilistic.

It is not destiny.

## The disturbing truth

Justice can have borders.

## What this does NOT prove

Exclusion never excuses harm.
