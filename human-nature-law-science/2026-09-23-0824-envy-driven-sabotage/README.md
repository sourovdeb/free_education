## The buried mechanism

Someone else rises.

Your rank feels lower.

Comparison becomes painful.

The target feels relevant.

Their gain feels personal.

Envy can then emerge.

Two routes may follow.

One route improves self.

Another lowers rivals.

That second route matters.

Sabotage can stay subtle.

Information may be withheld.

Credit may be redirected.

Help may preserve dependence.

Rumors may spread.

Mistakes may be amplified.

Cooperation may quietly shrink.

No violence is required.

## The central conflict

Morality sees character.

Science sees mechanisms.

Law sees proven conduct.

Investigators seek motives.

These frames can clash.

## The disturbing truth

**Success can invite punishment.**

Sometimes nothing was stolen.

Sometimes no rule broke.

Another person's advantage hurts.

That pain can motivate harm.

## What envy means

Envy concerns another's advantage.

Jealousy concerns threatened bonds.

The terms often blur.

Research separates them carefully.

Envy needs comparison.

Similarity often strengthens comparison.

Relevance also strengthens comparison.

Perceived unfairness can matter.

Perceived deservingness can matter.

Control beliefs also matter.

## What experiments found

Moran induced upward comparison.

Envy then increased deception.

Two studies supported this.

The setting was negotiation.

The effect was contextual.

Montal-Rosenberg studied helping.

Four studies were reported.

Envy reduced autonomous helping.

Dependency-preserving help increased.

The harm stayed deniable.

Zizzo studied money burning.

Participants paid personal costs.

They reduced others' money.

Rank predicted targeting patterns.

Deservingness also mattered.

This was not pure envy.

That distinction matters greatly.

## Workplace field evidence

Duffy tested social undermining.

Two studies were used.

One followed hospital workers.

Another followed student teams.

Envy predicted undermining pathways.

Moral disengagement mediated effects.

Team context changed strength.

Cohen-Charash tested unfairness.

Two studies were reported.

Envy predicted counterproductive conduct.

Unfairness strengthened that link.

## Why this unsettles

We prefer clean motives.

We praise merit systems.

We expect fair competition.

We condemn open sabotage.

Hidden sabotage feels different.

It can look normal.

It can look helpful.

It can look accidental.

That ambiguity protects actors.
