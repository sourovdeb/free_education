# Page 1 — Mechanism

## What is it?

Researchers study identifiability.

A victim gets named.
A face becomes visible.
A story becomes concrete.

Statistical victims stay abstract.

Numbers represent real people.
Yet emotion may weaken.

This creates asymmetry.

One person feels immediate.
Many people feel distant.

## Early evidence

Small and Loewenstein tested this.

Identification stayed minimal.
Personal details stayed limited.

Yet identified targets mattered.

Later experiments personalized victims.
Names and photographs appeared.

Kogut and Ritov tested singularity.

One identified victim gained.
Identified groups gained less.

Emotional distress also increased.

That suggested affective mediation.

Small and colleagues followed.

They studied charitable giving.
They compared victim formats.

Deliberation changed responses.

Awareness reduced one bias.
But total giving dropped.

Reason did not compensate.

That result became famous.

## Meta-analysis

Lee and Feeley reviewed.

They included 41 effects.

The average effect stayed small.
Their estimate was r=.05.

Heterogeneity was substantial.

Single victims mattered more.
Child images mattered more.
Low blame mattered more.
Donation contexts mattered more.

So context mattered greatly.

## Replication challenge

Later work challenged confidence.

Maier and colleagues reanalyzed.
They used bias adjustments.

The pooled effect weakened.

They then preregistered replication.
The sample reached 1,004.

Hypothetical donations were used.

The classic effect disappeared.
Bayesian tests favored nulls.

This matters greatly.

The effect is conditional.
It is not universal.

Real donations may differ.
Hypothetical choices may differ.

More replications remain needed.

## Why discomfort follows

We praise equal concern.

Yet attention stays unequal.

One face can dominate.
Large totals can fade.

This challenges self-image.

We think compassion scales.
Often it does not.

We think justice scales.
Emotion can distort scaling.

## The disturbing truth

**The disturbing truth:** Faces can outweigh headcounts.

## What this excludes

**What this does NOT prove:** Statistics should replace compassion.

It proves no cruelty.
It proves no inevitability.
It proves no selfishness.
It proves no fixed trait.

Identifiability can help.
Stories can mobilize care.
Names restore human presence.

The problem is weighting.
