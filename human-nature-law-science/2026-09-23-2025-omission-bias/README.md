# Page 1 — The mechanism

## Definition

Omission bias favors inaction.

Harmful acts feel worse.

Harmful omissions feel softer.

Outcomes may match.

Blame often does not.

This bias is documented.

It is not universal.

Context changes its strength.

## The disturbing truth

**The disturbing truth:**

Inaction can feel innocent.

## Basic mechanism

A bad outcome appears.

Two routes caused it.

One route involved action.

One route involved inaction.

People compare both routes.

Action attracts more blame.

Inaction attracts less blame.

Causation feels weaker.

Intent feels less visible.

Responsibility can shrink.

## Foundational evidence

Spranca studied this pattern.

Motives were held constant.

Consequences were held constant.

Acts drew harsher judgments.

Omissions drew softer judgments.

Causation judgments shifted too.

The study was published.

Its DOI remains stable.

Ritov studied vaccination choices.

Participants faced hypothetical risks.

Action carried small risk.

Inaction carried larger risk.

Many still avoided action.

That pattern showed bias.

Later field work followed.

Pertussis decisions were studied.

Omission bias still mattered.

Risk beliefs also mattered.

## Replication evidence

A meta-analysis exists.

It was preregistered.

Twenty-one samples were included.

Thirteen articles were included.

Forty-nine effects were tested.

The pooled effect persisted.

Reported g was 0.45.

The interval was wide.

Blame effects were stronger.

Decision effects were weaker.

Publication tests were mixed.

Some bias remained possible.

## What scientists dispute

Effect sizes vary.

Scenarios matter greatly.

Norms also matter.

Duties also matter.

Intentions also matter.

Status quo matters.

Normality can dominate omission.

Legal experiments show this.

Prentice tested legal judgments.

Normality influenced blame strongly.

Omission mattered much less.

That limits broad claims.

## Counterargument

Omissions can be worse.

Duties can reverse judgments.

Parents create clear duties.

Professionals create clear duties.

Promises create clear duties.

Created risks create duties.

So omission is contextual.

It is no excuse.

## What this proves

It shows judgment asymmetry.

It shows causal intuitions.

It shows responsibility shifts.

## What this does NOT prove

**What this does NOT prove:**

Inaction is always selfish.

It proves no determinism.

It proves no evil.

It proves no diagnosis.

# Page 2 — Five lenses

## 1. Psychology

Humans track visible action.

Visible acts feel causal.

Invisible nonaction feels diffuse.

That changes blame.

Emotion may amplify differences.

Anticipated regret also matters.

Responsibility avoidance may matter.

Status quo can reinforce it.

No single cause suffices.

### Why this hurts

People praise restraint.

Sometimes restraint causes harm.

People condemn intervention.

Sometimes intervention reduces harm.

Our self-image prefers innocence.

Inaction protects that image.

That protection can mislead.

Consider the St. Louis.

Cuba denied most entry.

The ship returned east.

European states admitted passengers.

Later outcomes were severe.

USHMM documents these facts.

### Interpretation

This is not proof.

No bias diagnosis follows.

Politics shaped those choices.

Administrative inaction mattered materially.

The case raises questions.

Does refusal feel passive?

Does passivity reduce blame?

History cannot answer alone.

Psychology supplies hypotheses only.
