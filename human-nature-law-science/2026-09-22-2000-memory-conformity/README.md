# Human Nature — Memory Conformity

## Page 1 — The trait

Memory can change socially.

This is memory conformity.

Another person speaks.

Their detail enters recall.

The witness stays sincere.

The memory still changes.

This matters in court.

Two witnesses may agree.

But sources may merge.

Agreement can look independent.

It may not be.

### Why humans do this

Memory is reconstructive.

Recall rebuilds past events.

Social information enters rebuilding.

Trusted people affect recall.

Repeated details gain familiarity.

Source memory can blur.

People confuse origins.

That creates false certainty.

### Core evidence

Gabbert and colleagues tested witnesses.

Pairs saw different videos.

They discussed the event.

Many imported new details.

Roediger tested social contagion.

Confederates supplied false objects.

Participants later recalled them.

Later studies tested faces.

Social answers changed recognition.

Confidence also changed sometimes.

These effects are probabilistic.

They do not prove contamination.

They show a mechanism.

## Page 2 — Law conflicts

Law needs usable testimony.

Courts assess witness credibility.

Science separates two questions.

Is the witness honest?

Is the memory accurate?

Those are not identical.

A sincere witness errs.

Cross-examination tests consistency.

It cannot restore encoding.

US doctrine draws boundaries.

Police suggestion matters greatly.

Other suggestion can matter less.

Science uses broader causation.

Peers can contaminate memory.

Media can contaminate memory.

Repeated questioning can matter.

New Jersey changed procedure.

Memory research influenced reforms.

England uses separation rules.

Early descriptions get recorded.

Witness communication gets limited.

### The conflict

Law seeks final decisions.

Science reports changing probabilities.

Law handles individual cases.

Science studies groups.

Law asks admissibility questions.

Science maps causal risks.

Both systems need translation.

### What remains disputed

Confidence is not worthless.

Timing changes its meaning.

Initial confidence may help.

Later confidence can drift.

Laboratory studies simplify reality.

Real crimes add stress.

Relationships also change effects.

No marker proves contamination.
