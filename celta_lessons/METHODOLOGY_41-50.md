## Lesson 41 · Writing Effective Lesson Aims · Teacher Training

**Personal Hook:** In feedback after a lesson observation, the first question is always: 'What was your main aim?' If you can't answer in one clear sentence, the lesson was unclear. I had to learn this during 120 supervised teaching hours.

**The Formula:** *By the end of the lesson, learners will be better able to [specific verb] [specific language/skill].*

**Key words:** *by the end* (measurable) · *learners* not 'students will be taught' · *better able to* (acknowledges prior knowledge) · specific verb: use, describe, write, distinguish — NOT 'understand' or 'know'

| Weak | Strong |
|------|--------|
| Students will learn about the present perfect. | Learners will be better able to use the present perfect to talk about life experiences. |
| To teach vocabulary about food. | Learners will be better able to use 8 food adjectives to describe dishes in spoken English. |

## Lesson 42 · CCQs vs ICQs · Teacher Training

**Personal Hook:** My trainer said it in week one: 'Do you understand?' is the least useful question in a classroom. The only honest answer is 'yes' — because 'no' is embarrassing.

**CCQs (Concept Checking Questions):** Check *meaning*. Must be simple (yes/no or forced choice). Learner can only answer correctly if they understood.
- Target: *'She was exhausted.'*
- CCQs: Did she have energy? (No.) Was she very tired? (Yes.)

**ICQs (Instruction Checking Questions):** Check *task understanding* before an activity.
- Instruction: 'Work in pairs. Circle THREE problems.'
- ICQ: 'How many problems?' (Three.) 'Alone or in pairs?' (Pairs.)

## Lesson 43 · Eliciting · Teacher Training

**Personal Hook:** The best moment in any class I taught was when a learner produced the target language before I'd written it on the board. That is eliciting.

**Techniques:**
| Technique | Example |
|-----------|----------|
| Visuals | Picture of someone running → 'What is she doing?' |
| Context + pause | 'She jogs every morning. She is very ___.' [pause] |
| Mime | Act out 'exhausted' |
| Synonym | 'We say 'big' informally. More formal?' → 'substantial' |
| Half-word | 'She was exh___.' [wait] |

## Lesson 44 · Error Correction · Teacher Training

**Personal Hook:** When I was learning French, the best teachers let me talk first and corrected me after. The worst interrupted mid-sentence — I'd lose the word, the idea, and the confidence.

| Type | When | How |
|------|------|-----|
| On-the-spot | Accuracy tasks (drills, controlled practice) | Reformulation / prompting |
| Delayed | Fluency tasks (discussion, roleplay) | Note errors → board anonymously → self-correct |

**Techniques:** Reformulation ('You went yesterday?') · Finger correction · Facial expression · Echo question ('She don't?' [pause])

## Lesson 45 · Lesson Staging: PPP · Teacher Training

**Personal Hook:** Observation feedback I received: 'The activities were good, but I couldn't see why they were in that order.' A lesson is a sequence, not a pile of good tasks.

| Stage | Purpose | Example |
|-------|---------|----------|
| Presentation | Introduce + contextualise TL | Story with the structure; elicit; board form; CCQs |
| Controlled Practice | Accuracy — use with support | Gap-fill, matching, transformation |
| Freer Production | Fluency — use communicatively | Roleplay, discussion, personalised writing |

## Framework Reference

- **PPP**: Presentation → Practice → Production (accuracy focus, lower levels)
- **TBL**: Task-Based Learning (Willis) — fluency focus, B1+
- **TTT**: Test → Teach → Test (diagnose first)
- **ESA**: Engage → Study → Activate (Harmer)
- **MFP/MFPA**: Meaning · Form · Pronunciation (· Appropriacy)
