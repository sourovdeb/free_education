## Lesson 16 · Wine and Drinks Vocabulary: From Bitters to Barolo · B1

**Personal Hook:** I trained as a sommelier at Uccello and Establishment — two of Merivale's restaurants in Sydney CBD. Guests would ask for 'something dry, not too oaky' and I had to decode that while opening the bottle.

**Key Taste Descriptors:**

| Word | Meaning | Use it for |
|------|---------|------------|
| dry | little or no sweetness | most red wine, champagne brut |
| full-bodied | rich, heavy texture | Barolo, Shiraz |
| oaky | vanilla/wood notes from barrel | Chardonnay, Rioja |
| crisp | high acidity, refreshing | Sauvignon Blanc, Chablis |
| tannic | drying sensation on gums | young Cabernet, Nebbiolo |

## Lesson 17 · Words for Feelings: Beyond Happy and Sad · A2/B2

**Personal Hook:** When I arrived in Sydney in 2005, I could say I was 'happy' or 'sad'. That was it. There is a difference between *homesick* and *lonely*, between *overwhelmed* and *exhausted*.

**Emotion Vocabulary by Intensity:**

| Feeling | Weaker | Stronger |
|---------|--------|----------|
| happy | pleased, content | delighted, elated |
| sad | disappointed, low | devastated, heartbroken |
| worried | uneasy | anxious, terrified |
| angry | irritated | furious, livid |
| tired | weary, drained | exhausted, burnt out |

**Nuanced (B2):** nostalgic · overwhelmed · apprehensive · relieved · homesick

## Lesson 18 · Work and Career Vocabulary · B1/B2

**Personal Hook:** I have had job interviews on three continents — in English, in French, once in broken Creole in Réunion.

**Essential Collocations:**
- apply **for** a job · gain **experience** · manage **a team** · develop **skills** · be responsible **for** · achieve **targets**

**Interview language:** "In my previous role, I was responsible for..." · "I developed strong communication skills through..." · "My greatest achievement was managing..."

## Lesson 19 · False Friends: English vs French · B1/B2

**Personal Hook:** I told my neighbour I was *embarrassed* using the French equivalent. In French, *embarrassé* means 'bothered/cluttered'. In English, *embarrassed* means red-faced with shame.

| English | Looks like (French) | French meaning | English meaning |
|---------|--------------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| embarrassed | embarrassé | bothered | ashamed |
| actually | actuellement | currently | in fact |
| sensible | sensible | sensitive | showing judgement |
| library | librairie | bookshop | place to borrow books |
| eventually | éventuellement | possibly | in the end |

## Lesson 20 · Describing Places · A2/B1

**Personal Hook:** I have called four places home: Chittagong, Sydney, Plessis-Trévise, and Réunion. Réunion is not just 'nice' — it is *lush*, *volcanic*, *remote*, *tropical*.

**Useful Adjectives:**
- Size/density: vast, compact, sprawling, cramped, spacious
- Atmosphere: vibrant, tranquil, bustling, sleepy, chaotic
- Geography: coastal, mountainous, volcanic, landlocked, tropical
- Character: multicultural, historic, industrial, touristy, authentic
- Feeling: welcoming, isolating, familiar, foreign, overwhelming
