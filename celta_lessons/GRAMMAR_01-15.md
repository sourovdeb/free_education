## Lesson 1 · Present Perfect vs Past Simple: When Time Matters · B1/B2

**Personal Hook:** When I arrived in Sydney in 2005, my English was, in my own words, *horrible*. A customer once asked: "Have you ever worked in hospitality?" I said, "Yes, I worked." Wrong. I should have said, "Yes, I have worked." That tiny difference cost me the job.

**The Key Difference:**
- **Past Simple** = finished time. *I worked at the Ivy last year.*
- **Present Perfect** = no specific time, or connected to now. *I have worked in three countries.*

**Examples:** She **moved** to Réunion *in 2023*. ✔ · She **has lived** in three countries. ✔

**Practice:** Fill in: lived / has never tried / arrived / has worked
1. I ___ in Sydney from 2005 to 2017.
2. She ___ sushi.
3. We ___ in Paris last January.
4. He ___ in restaurants for ten years — and still does.

**Answers:** lived · has never tried · arrived · has worked

## Lesson 2 · Modal Verbs for Advice · B1

**Personal Hook:** My first mentor at Merivale said: "You *should* taste before you pour. You *had better* know your list. And you *ought to* remember every regular."

| Modal | Strength | Use |
|-------|----------|-----|
| should | Neutral | You should practise daily. |
| ought to | Slightly formal | You ought to revise. |
| had better | Urgent/warning | You had better arrive on time. |

**Form:** should / ought to / had better + **base verb** (no *to* after *had better*)

**Practice:** Rewrite: (1) It's a good idea to drink water. [should] (2) I'm warning you — don't be late. [had better] (3) It's advisable to call first. [ought to]

**Answers:** (1) You should drink water. (2) You had better not be late. (3) You ought to call first.

## Lesson 3 · The Passive Voice · B1/B2

**Personal Hook:** At Star Casino, every movement was monitored. The wine *was chilled* before service. Tables *were cleared* within sixty seconds. The passive is the language of professional kitchens.

**Form:** be (correct tense) + past participle
- Present: The room **is cleaned** every morning.
- Past: The order **was placed** at noon.
- Modal: Guests **must be greeted** immediately.

**Use:** agent is unknown, obvious, or unimportant; formal/professional writing.

**Practice → Answers:**
1. They clean the kitchen every night. → The kitchen is cleaned every night.
2. Someone stole his bag. → His bag was stolen.
3. The chef has prepared your table. → Your table has been prepared.

## Lesson 4 · Articles: A, An, The · A2/B1

**Personal Hook:** "I want *a* water." I said this my first week in Sydney. Bengali has no articles — so this was invisible grammar made visible the hard way.

| Article | When | Example |
|---------|------|---------|
| a / an | First mention; one of many | I saw *a* restaurant. |
| the | Both speaker & listener know which | *The* restaurant was full. |
| — (zero) | General / uncountable / plural | *Water* is essential. |

**Tips:** *a* before consonant sound (a *u*niversity — /j/); *an* before vowel sound (an *h*our — silent h)

**Practice → Answers:** (1) ___ advice → — (uncountable) (2) ___ honest woman → an (3) ___ sun → The (4) ___ coffee, ___ orange juice → a coffee, an orange juice

## Lesson 5 · Relative Clauses · B1/B2

**Personal Hook:** Réunion is the island *where* my daughter was born. The nurse *who* handled the C-section had been working since 4am. Relative clauses pack a whole story into one sentence.

| Pronoun | Refers to |
|---------|-----------|
| who / that | People |
| which / that | Things & animals |
| where | Places |
| whose | Possession |

**Defining** (no commas): The book *that* I read changed my life.
**Non-defining** (commas, use *which* not *that*): Réunion, *which* is a French island, is stunning.

**Practice → Answers:**
1. That is the restaurant. I worked there for three years. → That is the restaurant **where** I worked for three years.
2. She is the manager. Her patience I admired. → She is the manager **whose** patience I admired.
