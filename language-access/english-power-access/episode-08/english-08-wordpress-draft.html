Orwell and political language

Words can hide responsibility.

Why Orwell cared

Politics shaped his writing.

He opposed totalitarianism.

He supported democratic socialism.

He wanted truthful art.

The 1946 essay

Horizon published the essay.

Orwell examined political prose.

He found repeated vagueness.

He blamed ready phrases.

The feedback loop

Foolish thought weakens language.

Weak language blurs thought.

Bad habits then spread.

Orwell called this reversible.

How words conceal

Euphemisms soften violent actions.

Abstractions hide affected people.

Passive phrases may hide agents.

Concrete verbs restore responsibility.

Six working rules

  1. Drop stale figures.
  2. Prefer shorter words.
  3. Cut spare words.
  4. Prefer active voice.
  5. Replace needless jargon.
  6. Break rules when needed.

Later evidence

Metaphors can steer choices.

Placement changed measured effects.

Participants missed that influence.

Evidence remains context-bound.

Important criticism

Plain prose can lie.

Passive voice can help.

Fresh metaphors can clarify.

Abstract terms can matter.

Rules never prove truth.

Reading test

Who acts?

What happened?

Who suffers?

Which facts verify?
