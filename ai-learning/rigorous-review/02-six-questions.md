# Try six questions for a more defensible answer

You could use these questions when a claim needs more than an immediate opinion. They are meant to make an answer inspectable, not authoritative-sounding.

| Heading | Question | Why include it? |
|---|---|---|
| Bottom line | What is the most defensible answer? | Gives the conclusion without hiding its scope. |
| Basis | Which evidence and considerations support it? | Makes support inspectable. |
| Main uncertainty | What objection, alternative, or missing information matters? | Identifies the gap most likely to change the answer. |
| Confidence | How strong is the support, and why? | Separates justified confidence from confident wording. |
| Next action | What is the most appropriate feasible step? | Turns analysis into one practical choice. |
| Reconsider when | What evidence, event, or argument would change the answer? | Makes revision possible. |

## A synthetic example

**Bottom line:** 60% of the supplied forms were complete.

**Basis:** 12 ÷ 20 × 100 = 60%, calculated from the supplied counts.

**Main uncertainty:** whether those counts cover the intended collection.

**Confidence:** High for the arithmetic, conditional on the counts.

**Next action:** use 60% for this 20-form set.

**Reconsider when:** corrected counts or a different denominator change the fraction.

This is an authored example from the package, not a separate model's observed answer. Straightforward arithmetic does not need invented controversy.

## Choose the source relationship

You could select SOURCE_ONLY for faithful reporting or evaluation of supplied material, VERIFY for checking claims against permitted independent/current sources, or EXPAND for clearly labelled research and proposals.

**Why:** a faithful summary should not silently become a correction, while verification requires more than paraphrasing. Inferences from supplied data remain labelled as inferences.

The package proposes High, Moderate, Low or Insufficient confidence, followed by reasons. A narrow calculation can have strong support while an overall conclusion remains unresolved. An invented percentage is not calibration.

Six headings do not require six long sections. A simple answer can stay short; a substantial review may need a separate ledger.

## Limit

The structure does not certify truth. An answer can use every heading while citing the wrong passage. Recommending an action is also different from executing it.

**Next step to try:** apply the headings to one claim and inspect the support, assumption or inference behind every decisive sentence.
