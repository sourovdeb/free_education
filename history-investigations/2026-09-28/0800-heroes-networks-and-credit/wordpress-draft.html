Rosa Parks was prepared

Was one refusal enough?

Documented fact. Rosa Parks joined Montgomery’s NAACP. That happened during 1943. She served as secretary. Her work included investigations. She documented racial violence. During August 1955, she attended Highlander. Its workshop taught desegregation organizing. Parks refused relocation later. Police arrested her December 1. The Women’s Political Council mobilized. It announced the first boycott. Local churches supported transport. The Montgomery Improvement Association formed. Lawyers pursued *Browder v. Gayle*. Earlier resisters included Claudette Colvin. Scholarly interpretation. Parks’s arrest became catalytic. Her standing aided mobilization. Prepared networks converted defiance. A lone gesture did not. Benefits. One person clarified injustice. Her case unified action. Counterevidence and limits. Parks acted from conviction. Evidence shows no staged arrest. Earlier cases faced obstacles. Organizers made strategic choices. Public memory simplified coordination. That memory preserves courage. It can erase colleagues. The boycott required thousands. Legal victory used several plaintiffs. History needs both levels.

Wartime research then expanded. British findings added urgency. American entry changed scale. The Army created Manhattan District. Scholarly interpretation. Einstein supplied public authority. Szilard supplied initiative. The signature opened presidential attention. It did not design bombs. Benefits. Early warning funded research. Fear addressed genuine uncertainty. Counterevidence and limits. German capability was overestimated. The letter was catalytic. It was not sufficient. Later science mattered more. Military organization mattered too. Industrial production became decisive. Calling Einstein the creator misstates causation. Removing the letter changes timing. It cannot prove failure.

Note G described Bernoulli numbers. It gave sequenced operations. The Analytical Engine remained unfinished. Therefore, this program never ran. Babbage’s notebooks contained algorithms. Their collaboration shaped the publication. Lovelace also imagined wider uses. Numbers could represent symbols. They could represent music. Scholarly interpretation. Note G became foundational. It is often called first. That label depends upon definitions. Published algorithm differs from execution. Programming also predates electronic computers. Benefits. The label restores visibility. It expands computing history. Counterevidence and limits. Lovelace did not build hardware. Babbage supplied machine architecture. He had sketched procedures earlier. Their correspondence shows collaboration. Individual credit remains debatable. Her general-purpose insight stands. Calling her merely translator fails. Calling her sole originator fails too.

Her role lacked due recognition. Recent scholarship rejects victim caricatures. It presents an equal contributor. Benefits. Corrected history restores credit. Collaboration becomes visible. Evidence does not prove hero worship. Coordination explains attention. It cannot justify monopoly credit.
