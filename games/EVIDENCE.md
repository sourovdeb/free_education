# Browser Games: Evidence and Limits

Checked 2026-10-05. Editions 001 and 002.

## What the activities do

Step Quest offers example-sequence practice. Pattern Relay offers matching or voluntary recall. Visible examples remove the need to memorise their contents. This is an accommodation, not evidence of increased memory capacity. These prototypes have no participant trials. Transfer to everyday functioning and clinical outcomes remain untested. Do not describe either as treatment, diagnosis, or an autism intervention.

## Primary study: de Vries and colleagues

Marieke de Vries, Pier J. M. Prins, Ben Schmand, Hilde M. Geurts (2015). **Working memory and cognitive flexibility-training for children with an autism spectrum disorder: a randomized controlled trial.** Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry 56(5), 566–576. Published online in 2014.

DOI: https://doi.org/10.1111/jcpp.12324
Publisher: https://acamh.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jcpp.12324

Design: randomized comparison of adaptive working-memory training, adaptive cognitive-flexibility training, and nonadaptive mock training. Participants: 121 children, ages 8–12, IQ above 80. Tests before, after, and six weeks after training. Outcomes included trained/related executive tasks and parent-reported daily functioning. Attrition: 26%. Completers improved on several measures, but significant differential intervention effects were not established. Within-group improvement alone does not establish treatment efficacy.

Limits: the intervention differs from both prototypes. Attrition complicates interpretation. Child findings cannot establish adult benefits. This review inspected the publisher's indexed abstract; direct publisher access returned 403, and PubMed presented a browser check. The full paper was not reviewed during this delivery. No claims about uninspected methods or effect sizes are made.

## Official accessibility guidance

W3C WAI (2021), **Ensure Processes Do Not Rely on Memory**. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/objectives/o6-memory/

Inspected HTML guidance. Supports keeping instructions and needed information available. This is supplementary cognitive-accessibility guidance, not an efficacy trial or a WCAG conformance certificate.

W3C WAI (2021), **Limit Interruptions**. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG2/supplemental/patterns/o5p01-minimal-interruptions/

Inspected HTML guidance. Informs the absence of unsolicited sound, motion, and timed interruptions. The prototype implementation still needs usability assessment.

## Autistic-led design source

Autistic Self Advocacy Network, **One Idea Per Line: A Guide to Making Easy Read Resources**. https://autisticadvocacy.org/resources/accessibility/easyread/

Inspected the site's indexed introduction; a direct fetch returned 403. The full downloadable guide was not reviewed. The introduction describes accessible language and developing materials with self-advocates. It informs our invitation for player feedback, not a claim of ASAN endorsement or co-design. These games are not certified Easy Read resources.

## Claims we do not make

No guaranteed learning, attention, executive-function, mood, or memory gains. No clinical validation. No generalization from autism to a fixed set of preferences. No claim that ordinary gameplay results measure ability. No claim that an author reviewing the interface substitutes for autistic-player participation.
