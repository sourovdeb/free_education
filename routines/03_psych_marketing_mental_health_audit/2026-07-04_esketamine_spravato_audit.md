# News Item: Does Esketamine (Spravato) Really Work for Treatment-Resistant Depression?

**Date:** 2026-07-04
**Category:** Mental Health Treatment / Pharma Funding Audit
**Type:** Claim audit (psychology & mental-health treatment desk)

---

## The Micro-Blog Post

**The claim:** Spravato (esketamine nasal spray), approved by the FDA in 2019 as a "breakthrough" rapid-acting treatment for treatment-resistant depression (TRD), is often marketed and cited as a major advance over standard antidepressants.

**What the evidence actually shows:** Of the three short-term, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials Janssen submitted to the FDA, only **one** showed a statistically significant benefit over placebo — a roughly 4-point improvement on a 60-point depression scale (KFF Health News, 2022). To get to two positive studies, the FDA controversially counted a relapse-prevention trial as an efficacy trial and loosened its own TRD criteria mid-review. Independent psychiatrists not tied to Janssen — including former FDA reviewer Erick Turner and Iowa's Jess Fiedorowicz — say the benefit was "almost certainly exaggerated," and the UK's NICE declined to recommend the drug after its own independent review (Mad in America, 2022; KFF Health News, 2022).

**Funding and conflicts of interest:** Every pivotal trial was designed, run, and funded by Janssen Research & Development (Johnson & Johnson), which also holds the patent and sets the ~$4,700/month price. When the independent Cochrane review raised doubts about ketamine-class drugs' evidence base, the rebuttal defending esketamine was authored entirely by Janssen employees who are also J&J stockholders (PubMed 36218274) — a direct financial stake in a favorable verdict, undisclosed in most press coverage.

**Verdict:** Contested, not robust. The one positive trial and heavy FDA discretion mean esketamine's "breakthrough" reputation outruns its independently verified evidence. Cheaper generic IV ketamine, which nobody profits from patenting, hasn't gotten comparable trial investment — a funding asymmetry worth flagging whenever treatment comparisons are made.

## Audit Notes (methodology, for transparency)

- **Sample size / methodology:** Pivotal TRD program covered ~1,700 adults across multiple short-term RCTs; only 1 of 3 short-term trials reached statistical significance vs. placebo.
- **Funding trace:** 100% industry-funded pivotal trials (Janssen/J&J). The main published rebuttal to independent Cochrane skepticism was itself authored by Janssen employees/shareholders — a direct conflict of interest.
- **Opposing/neutral evidence:** UK NICE (public health body, no commercial stake) reviewed the same evidence base independently and did not recommend esketamine for NHS use. Independent academic psychiatrists (Turner, Fiedorowicz, Schatzberg) have published critical reanalyses.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high that the "breakthrough" framing overstates the independently verified evidence; this is a well-documented, multi-source-corroborated controversy rather than a single contested study.
