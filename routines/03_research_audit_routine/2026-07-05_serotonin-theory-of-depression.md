# Audit: Does Low Serotonin Cause Depression?

**Claim under review:** The "chemical imbalance" story — that depression is caused by low serotonin — has been the default public explanation for depression for 30+ years, echoed in SSRI marketing and pop psychology.

**The strongest test of the claim:** In July 2022, a team led by Joanna Moncrieff published an umbrella review in *Molecular Psychiatry*, pooling 17 systematic reviews and meta-analyses across six research lines — serotonin/5-HIAA body-fluid levels, 5-HT1A receptor binding, serotonin transporter (SERT) levels, tryptophan-depletion studies, and a SERT gene-association study covering 115,257 people. Their conclusion: no consistent evidence that depression is caused by lowered serotonin concentration or activity. ([Full paper, open access](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10618090/))

**Funding and conflicts:** The paper states there was no specific funding for the review. One co-author (Mark Horowitz) co-founded a company that helps patients taper off antidepressants; another draws book royalties from antidepressant-skeptic writing — financial interests aligned with the paper's conclusion. Worth disclosing, not automatically disqualifying.

**The pushback:** In 2023, 36 researchers led by Sameer Jauhar published a comment in the same journal calling the review methodologically flawed — misapplied quality criteria, selective interpretation, "antiquated" neuroscience framing. ([Reply, Molecular Psychiatry 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-023-02093-0)) A separate commentary alleges some critics have undisclosed pharma industry ties, but that claim comes from an advocacy outlet and wasn't independently itemized here — treat it as unverified.

**Bottom line:** The narrow finding — no single serotonin marker reliably tracks with depression — holds up and was already known among specialists; it's a real, if unsurprising, result. It does **not** show antidepressants don't work (a separate, better-supported question the review didn't test). The claim is contested in framing and interpretation, not cleanly "debunked," and gets oversimplified by marketing and headlines on both sides.

**Sources (independent of each other):**
