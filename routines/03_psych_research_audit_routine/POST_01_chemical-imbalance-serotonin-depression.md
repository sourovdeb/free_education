# Is Depression Really a "Chemical Imbalance"?

**Date:** 2026-07-05
**Category:** Mental health treatment / pharmaceutical marketing
**Status:** Audited — sources verified, funding and conflicts disclosed

---

## The micro-blog (news item)

Is depression really a "chemical imbalance"? For 30 years, SSRI ads and pharmacy
leaflets have said so — but the claim was pharma marketing more than science. It
traces to a 1960s hypothesis that drugmakers heavily promoted in direct-to-consumer
SSRI ads once the FDA loosened ad rules in the late 1970s; the American Psychiatric
Association even echoed it in a 2005 patient leaflet.

In 2022, Joanna Moncrieff's team (UCL/NHS, publicly funded, no pharma ties
disclosed) pooled 17 prior studies in a *Molecular Psychiatry* umbrella review and
found no consistent evidence that depression is caused by low serotonin
([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35854107/)).

Psychiatrists led by Sameer Jauhar (King's College London) rebutted it in 2023,
calling the review's methodology arbitrary
([*Journal of Psychopharmacology*](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02698811231161813);
[reply in *Molecular Psychiatry*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-023-02093-0)).
Their methodological points deserve real weight — but several signatories disclosed
pages of pharmaceutical financial ties, and one is CEO of a company developing its
own serotonin drug, a direct stake in the outcome
([Mad in America](https://www.madinamerica.com/2023/07/pharma-ceo-others-attempt-contradictory-critiques-of-serotonin-debunking-study/)).

**Verdict:** the simple "chemical imbalance" story was never well-supported and was
amplified by parties selling the fix. Whether serotonin plays no role at all in
depression, as the 2022 review concludes, is more contested — some critics have
legitimate points, others have a product to protect. Read this as: the marketing
claim is debunked; the deeper science is unsettled, not closed.

This is a research-literacy summary, not medical advice — don't change medication
without a clinician.

## Audit trail

### 1. Core claim
"Depression is caused by a chemical imbalance — specifically low serotonin — and
SSRIs correct it." Popularized in mass-market advertising and patient materials
since the 1990s.

### 2. Primary sources & methodology
- **Moncrieff J, Cooper RE, Stockmann T, Amendola S, Hengartner MP, Horowitz MA.**
  "The serotonin theory of depression: a systematic umbrella review of the
  evidence." *Molecular Psychiatry*, 2022.
  https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35854107/
  — An umbrella review pooling **17 prior studies**: 12 systematic
  reviews/meta-analyses, 1 collaborative meta-analysis, 1 meta-analysis of large
  cohort studies, 1 systematic review/narrative synthesis, 1 genetic association
  study, 1 further umbrella review. It covers serotonin metabolite levels, receptor
  binding, transporter levels, tryptophan-depletion studies, and genetic
  associations. Authors: UCL Division of Psychiatry, North East London NHS
  Foundation Trust, University of Greenwich, Psychiatry-UK, Sapienza University of
  Rome, Zurich University of Applied Sciences.

- **Jauhar S, Cowen PJ, Browning M.** "Fifty years on: Serotonin and depression."
  *Journal of Psychopharmacology*, 2023.
  https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/02698811231161813
  — Rebuttal arguing the umbrella review's certainty-rating criteria were
  non-standard/arbitrary and that it summarized rather than re-analyzed underlying
  data.

- Formal reply/counter-reply thread in *Molecular Psychiatry*, 2023:
  https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-023-02093-0

### 3. Funding & conflicts of interest
- **Moncrieff et al. (2022):** No pharmaceutical industry funding disclosed.
  Moncrieff reports book royalties and unpaid leadership roles in psychiatric
  reform networks; Hengartner reports book royalties; **Horowitz co-founded a
  company assisting antidepressant withdrawal** (a smaller commercial interest,
  noted here for balance, disclosed by the authors themselves in April 2022);
  Cooper and Stockmann hold unpaid board positions in related advocacy
  organizations.
- **Critics (Jauhar et al. and co-signatories):** Per reporting by *Mad in
  America*, the group's published conflict-of-interest list runs to "several pages
  of small print" of pharmaceutical industry financial ties. One signatory, **Jacob
  Jacobsen, is founder/CEO of Evecxia Therapeutics**, a company developing its own
  serotonin-targeting antidepressant — a direct commercial stake in the serotonin
  hypothesis remaining credible.
  https://www.madinamerica.com/2023/07/pharma-ceo-others-attempt-contradictory-critiques-of-serotonin-debunking-study/
- Historical origin: the serotonin/"chemical imbalance" narrative was actively
  built out by SSRI manufacturers' direct-to-consumer marketing in the 1990s, and
  was repeated uncritically by the American Psychiatric Association in a 2005
  patient leaflet — i.e., the claim's popularization has an identifiable
  commercial funding trail independent of this specific 2022–2023 dispute.

### 4. Bias pattern check
The pattern is legible: parties with a financial stake in SSRIs continuing to be
prescribed on the "corrects a chemical imbalance" rationale are the same parties
disputing the review that undermines that rationale. That doesn't make their
methodological criticisms wrong — arbitrary certainty-grading is a fair scientific
concern — but it does mean their conclusions should be weighted with that conflict
in view, not taken as disinterested peer critique.

### 5. Independent/opposing comparison
- UCL press analysis (independent summary, not authored by parties to the dispute):
  https://www.ucl.ac.uk/news/2022/jul/analysis-depression-probably-not-caused-chemical-imbalance-brain-new-study
- Methodological commentary broadly agreeing the "chemical imbalance" *marketing*
  claim was always oversimplified, while noting umbrella reviews of this kind can
  themselves have "leaky" methodology and shouldn't be read as proof serotonin is
  irrelevant (PMC methodological reflections):
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9957868/

### 6. Verdict
Two separate questions get conflated in public discourse and need to stay
separate:

1. **"Was the popular 'chemical imbalance' explanation good science?"** — No.
   It was a simplification pushed hardest by companies selling the drugs it
   justified, and it was never well-supported even before 2022.
2. **"Does serotonin play any role in depression at all?"** — Genuinely contested.
   The 2022 umbrella review's stronger claim is disputed by researchers who raise
   real methodological issues, though several of them also have material financial
   interests in the opposite conclusion.

Given verified sources on both sides and disclosed conflicts on both sides, this
claim is **partially confirmed, partially contested** — not a clean "myth" or
"settled fact" either way.
