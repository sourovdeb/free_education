
# Routine 3 — Psychology & Mental Health Research Audit

**What it does:** Picks a widely-repeated claim about human psychology,
neuroscience, marketing influence, or mental health treatment; traces it to
primary research; checks who funded the research (pharma, insurance,
university, government); checks for bias in the conclusions; compares it
against independent or opposing research; and publishes a short, sourced
audit as a "news item" post.

**Location:** `routines/03_psych_research_audit_routine/`
**Frequency:** As needed
**Output format:** Markdown (one file per audited claim)
**Status:** ✅ Active

## Method (applied to every post)

1. Identify the core claim.
2. Locate primary sources; check methodology and sample size.
3. Investigate funding and conflicts of interest for every side of the debate.
4. Look for patterns where conclusions favor a funder's commercial or
   institutional interest.
5. Compare against independent/opposing research.
6. Publish a 200–300 word micro-blog with citations, funding disclosures,
   and a plain verdict on whether the claim holds up.

If sources can't be verified or bias can't be assessed, the post says so
explicitly rather than asserting confidence it doesn't have. Every post cites
at least two independent sources.

## Disclaimer

These posts are research-literacy summaries for a general audience, not
medical, legal, or financial advice. They do not tell anyone to start, stop,
or change a treatment — consult a qualified professional for that.
