## 1. Prepare the thought

Start with the listener. Decide what the speaker wants.
Name the obstacle. Choose a tactic, not an adjective.
Useful verbs: invite, test, conceal, reassure, challenge, dismiss, confess, delay.

Example source: "I'm glad you came."

| Context | Objective | Obstacle | Tactic | Focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| reunion | welcome the person | remaining disbelief | acknowledge | "came" |
| suspicion | discover their reason | distrust | test | "you" |
| hiding resentment | avoid a quarrel | hurt | conceal | "glad" with restraint |

The words remain unchanged. None is the guaranteed interpretation.
Choose the source-supported reading. Label the choice as CHOICE.
Don't manufacture irony without context.

## 2. Shape the turn

Treat the beat as a thought-action. A new tactic starts a beat.
Punctuation alone does not decide it. Neither does sentence length.
Use one main focus per thought. Let the surrounding words support it.

| Tool | What it can do | What it doesn't prove |
|---|---|---|
| Onset | enter willingly, reluctantly, or abruptly | a fixed emotion |
| Duration | let a recognition arrive | loudness |
| Pitch change | distinguish an idea or contrast | truth, sincerity, or identity |
| Articulation | make an instruction land | anger |
| Pace shift | press or delay a tactic | excitement alone |
| Silence | permit thought or listening | fear alone |
| Recovery | show control returning | emotion disappearing |

Avoid a cue on every word. Use no cue where none helps.
Choose a dominant instruction. Add a countercurrent only if useful.

### Beat card

| Item | Example |
|---|---|
| Source | "I thought I'd lost you." |
| Listener | the returned companion |
| Objective | acknowledge the fear |
| Obstacle | difficulty saying "lost" |
| Tactic | confess, then reconnect |
| Turn | "you" |
| Action cue | delay "lost"; release on "you" |
| Exit | finish without another sob |

This doesn't establish actual grief. The source supports several readings.
Remove hesitation if it sounds imposed.

## 3. Choose by situation

These are original rehearsal proposals. No chart determines human emotion.
Use them as alternatives, not prescriptions.

| Situation | Playable action | Delivery choice | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| fear while hiding | prevent discovery | contain onset; finish the thought | constant gasps or whisper strain |
| fear during rescue | make someone act | articulate the action-word; continue forward | dragging every word |
| anger while maintaining control | establish a boundary | land negation; settle the ending | compulsory shouting |
| anger after humiliation | protect status | delay the response; choose one focus | generic threatening accent |
| grief while resisting tears | complete the thought | one hesitation; recovery at phrase-end | forced crying, repeated breaks |
| grief after exhaustion | report what remains | fewer pitch changes; preserve words | every sentence trailing away |
| relief with remaining fear | reconnect after danger | release after recognition | joy before the reveal |
| delight shared privately | draw someone closer | direct attention; let the key word lift | permanent smiling tone |
| disbelief | test what was heard | hold the disputed word; listen afterward | every line rising |
| suspicion | invite information | withhold a finality cue until response | villain coding |
| irony | expose the mismatch | let literal wording remain plausible | sneering every syllable |
| comedy | allow recognition | protect setup; place space near turn | laughing before the listener |
| embarrassment | recover face | redirect focus; recover phrase rhythm | added stammers without reason |
| authority | obtain action | make the instruction legible | forced low pitch |
| intimacy | address one person | conversational level; retain consonants | sustained breathy whisper |
| wonder | discover | let the named detail change focus | awe on every noun |
| nonfiction explanation | make a relation clear | contrast premise and consequence | cinematic effects on every claim |
| first-person memoir | distinguish then and now | mark reflection versus remembered speech | inventing recalled dialogue |

## 4. Separate display and subtext

A person can say one thing. They can want something else.
They can resist their own feeling. The narrator need not expose everything.

Source: "Of course you can stay."

| Layer | Choice |
|---|---|
| Surface act | grant permission |
| Unspoken resistance | inconvenience or unresolved resentment |
| Audible display | finish the offer without sarcasm |
| Optional leak | slight delay before "stay" |
| Listener interpretation | remains uncertain |

Do not turn leaks into measurements. Do not diagnose motives from delivery.
One restrained cue is enough.

For ambivalence, use parallel emotion rows. Do not average them into one value.

| Anchor | Relief | Fear remaining | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| "thought" | trace | presence | approach the admission |
| "lost" | trace | presence | let the word arrive |
| "you" | presence | trace | reconnect |

The row is preparation only. Keep it outside performance scripts.
Inside scripts, give one playable direction.

## 5. Rehearse without over-scoring

Take A: preserve the thought. Take B: alter one chosen parameter.
Ask what changed in meaning. Keep the version serving context.
Do not judge by theatricality alone.

| Problem heard or reported | One revision |
|---|---|
| every line sounds important | remove most focus marks |
| emotion precedes discovery | move the turn after evidence |
| character vanishes during stress | restore accent/reset phrase first |
| pronunciation sounds word-by-word | practice the whole thought-group |
| joke is over-signalled | remove laugh and exaggeration |
| effects obscure the line | remove effect, then reconsider placement |
| recurring character drifts | rehearse return against saved baseline |

Without audio, phrase these as possibilities. Never pretend to hear the problem.
For actual feedback, cite the recording. Use timestamps only when observed.

Backchain when a phrase fails. Then return to the whole sentence.
Avoid drilling disconnected words forever.

Stop at discomfort. Replace the effect with speech.
For voice-care limits, see sources. This is not clinical treatment.
